# Phonology and tone source-alignment diagnostic

The safe conclusion remains narrow but now explicitly hybrid: use literature-backed orientation claims for the system-level account, and include a small number of Bible-attested minimal or near-minimal sets where the forms are source-resolved in corpus context.

## 1. Segmental phonology

The literature supports a conservative segmental summary rather than a full phoneme table. Henderson and ZNC support a small consonant inventory, a five-vowel summary, and simple syllable-shape claims suitable for grammar-facing orientation prose [@henderson1965, pp. 9-13; @zamngaihcing2017, pp. 25-26, 37].

| Area | Safe conclusion | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consonants | Conservative consonant inventory summary is safe to print. | safe |
| Vowels | Conservative vowel inventory summary is safe to print. | safe |
| Syllable shape | Mostly simple CV/CVC orientation is safe to print. | safe |
| Orthography | Practical spelling is useful for locating forms but remains indirect phonological evidence. | safe with caveat |

## 2. Tone

Tedim is described as a three-tone language in the literature, and ordinary spelling does not mark tone. The section can therefore state the three-tone summary, but should not claim that spelling alone recovers tone or that every tonal alternation is solved [@henderson1965, p. 13; @zamngaihcing2017, pp. 57-60].

A small number of Bible-attested examples are now appropriate where forms are recoverable and source-resolved:

- `ta / -ta` in `nungta` (Genesis 11:30; Matthew 4:4),
- `thei / -thei` in `lutthei` (Genesis 4:9; Matthew 7:21),
- `hi / hi` in `na hi hi` (Genesis 16:13).

These examples support the existence and contextual use of the forms. The tone analysis attached to them remains literature-backed when orthography does not mark tone directly.

## 3. The blocked `-a` issue

The analyzer-gap notes (`docs/grammar/README_ANALYZER_GAPS.md`, `docs/grammar/ANALYZER_LITERATURE_GAPS.md`, `docs/grammar/ANALYZER_GAPS_CORPUS_EXAMPLES.md`, `docs/grammar/ANALYZER_GAPS_QUICK_REFERENCE.md`) keep the high/low `-a` contrast blocked. The literature distinguishes the values, but the current corpus spelling does not preserve tone, so the distinction cannot be recovered safely.

Best-supported conclusion: `-a` remains blocked and unresolved, not normalized into a single grammar fact.

## 4. Relationship to other grammar sections

## 5. Print decision

### Safe to print now

- cautious segmental orientation
- orthography caveat
- three-tone status summary
- two or three Bible-attested minimal or near-minimal sets where source resolution is solid
- blocked `-a` warning

### Literature-only wording

- tone is contrastive
- ordinary spelling does not mark tone
- tone values for Bible-attested forms are literature-backed when orthography is unmarked

### Blocked

- the exact high/low `-a` distinction
- full tone sandhi rules
- any claim of a complete tone system

### Belongs elsewhere

- stem alternation
- TAM / aspect / modal
- `-pih`
- verb paradigms
