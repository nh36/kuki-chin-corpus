# Overview of verbal `-pih` comitative applicative

This section isolates verbal `-pih` as a comitative applicative suffix so that its function is no longer treated only as a boundary note within the derivation/valency account. The first claim is deliberately narrow: verbal `-pih` attaches to Form II / Stem 2 verbs and introduces or indexes a comitative participant or comitative object, increasing verb valence.

Verbal `-pih` must be kept distinct from nominal `-pih` meaning 'member / group member', which is documented independently by Henderson (1965) and illustrated in forms such as `innkuanpihte` 'family members' and `mipihte` 'group members / companions'. This section does not claim a full applicative chapter, a full valency chapter, a full derivational morphology chapter, a full VP-slot template, or a complete comitative system. It also does not settle the status of high-frequency forms such as `hopih`, `hehpih`, or `ompih`, or the valency interaction between `-pih` and `-sak`, `-san`, directionals, or heavier VP stacks.

# Verbal `-pih` as comitative applicative

The clearest current evidence for verbal `-pih` as a comitative applicative is `paipih` 'go with / accompany / bring'. The form is transparent, productive across the corpus, and consistent with the Otsuka (2011) analysis of `-pih` as a comitative applicative that attaches to Form II / Stem 2 verbs and introduces a new core argument representing either a comitative participant or a comitative object.

In Genesis the form appears as God bringing the woman to the man, introducing the woman as the accompanying participant.

(@ex:pih-core-paipih-gen2) Genesis 2:22
a. Tedim: mipa kiangah tua nu a paipih hi.
b. Segmentation: mipa | kiang-ah | tua | nu | a | pai-pih | hi
c. Gloss: man | beside-LOC | DEM | woman | 3SG | go.II-COM.APPL | DECL
d. Translation: [God] brought her to the man. (Genesis 2:22)

The same form `paipih` appears in the Gospels in a motion-to-wilderness context where the Holy Spirit leads Jesus.

(@ex:pih-core-paipih-matt4) Matthew 4:1
a. Tedim: Kha Siangtho in gamlakah Jesuh a paipih hi.
b. Segmentation: Kha | Siangtho | in | gamlak-ah | Jesuh | a | pai-pih | hi
c. Gloss: Spirit | Holy | ERG | wilderness-LOC | Jesus | 3SG | go.II-COM.APPL | DECL
d. Translation: The Holy Spirit led Jesus into the wilderness. (Matthew 4:1)

These two examples are enough to establish `paipih` as a productive verbal comitative applicative form. They do not by themselves exhaust the system or settle every valency question involving `-pih`.

# Form II / Stem 2 restriction

The Form II restriction is established primarily by literature evidence. Otsuka (2011) states that comitative `-pih` attaches only to Form II verbs, and ZNC (2017/2018) independently documents this with explicit grammaticality contrasts. The corpus forms `paipih`, `nekpih`, and `tunpih` are all consistent with this restriction, since `pai`, `nek`, and `tun` appear here in their Stem 2 shapes. However, the corpus alone does not provide direct Form I vs Form II contrast pairs, so the restriction is best stated cautiously as a literature-established generalization that the corpus conforms to rather than as a corpus-proved rule.

This note is not a claim to have verified the restriction exhaustively. A human reviewer should check whether any counter-example exists in the wider corpus.

# Transitive verb contexts

The comitative applicative also attaches to transitive verbs, as shown by `nekpih` 'eat together with'. In a Proverbs context the form appears as an infinitival purpose clause where the speaker invites the addressee to share a meal.

(@ex:pih-trans-nekpih-prov7) Proverbs 7:15
a. Tedim: Ka nekpih dingin nang ka hong zong a, tu-in kong mu petmah hi.
b. Segmentation: ka | nek-pih | ding-in | nang | ka | hong | zong | a
c. Gloss: 1SG | eat.II-COM.APPL | NMLZ-ERG | 2SG | 1SG | VEN | search | CONJ
d. Translation: I came looking for you, to eat together with me. (Proverbs 7:15)

The Gospel counterpart shows the same `nekpih` in an invitation clause where Jesus is the comitative participant.

(@ex:pih-trans-nekpih-luke11) Luke 11:37
a. Tedim: Farisi mi khat in an nekpih dingin Jesuh a sam hi.
b. Segmentation: Farisi | mi | khat | in | an | nek-pih | ding-in | Jesuh | a | sam | hi
c. Gloss: Pharisee | person | one | ERG | food | eat.II-COM.APPL | NMLZ-ERG | Jesus | 3SG | invite | DECL
d. Translation: A Pharisee invited Jesus to eat with him. (Luke 11:37)

Both rows show `nekpih` in purposive or nominalized environments. Example quality is acceptable for a first grammar-facing claim even in these non-finite contexts.

# Motion verbs and comitative objects

Otsuka (2011) distinguishes two types of comitative argument introduced by `-pih`: a comitative participant (a person who goes along) and a comitative object (a thing that is brought along). The form `tunpih` 'arrive with / bring with / come with' illustrates both types.

In Genesis 8 the dove arrives with a freshly plucked olive leaf as comitative object.

(@ex:pih-motion-tunpih-gen8) Genesis 8:11
a. Tedim: oliv teh a tukkhiat thak khat a mukah petin hong tunpih hi.
b. Segmentation: oliv | teh | a | tukkhiat | thak | khat | a | mukah | petin | hong | tun-pih | hi
c. Gloss: olive | leaf | 3SG | pluck | fresh | one | 3SG | beak | hold.CVB | VEN | arrive.II-COM.APPL | DECL
d. Translation: [the dove] arrived with a freshly plucked olive leaf in its beak. (Genesis 8:11)

In Matthew 9 the same form `tunpih` introduces a comitative participant (the paralytic brought to Jesus).

(@ex:pih-motion-tunpih-matt9) Matthew 9:2
a. Tedim: Mi pawlkhat in khutlekhezaw mi khat a lupna pheek tawh hong pua-in Jesuh kiang a hong tunpih uh hi.
b. Segmentation: mi | pawlkhat | in | khutlekhezaw | mi | khat | Jesuh | kiang | a | hong | tun-pih | uh | hi
c. Gloss: person | some | ERG | paralytic | person | one | Jesus | beside | 3SG | VEN | arrive.II-COM.APPL | PL | DECL
d. Translation: Some people brought a paralytic to Jesus. (Matthew 9:2)

The contrast between these two rows keeps both comitative object and comitative participant uses visible without forcing a single analysis of all `tunpih` occurrences.

# Boundary with nominal `-pih`

Nominal `-pih` meaning 'member / group member' must remain a visible boundary in any grammar-facing account of verbal `-pih`. Henderson (1965) documents this suffix with examples such as `a inkuan-pih te` 'his family members', `ka kho-pih` 'my village-mate', and `na sang-pih` 'your school-mate'. The corpus contains over a hundred occurrences of `innkuanpihte` 'family members' and `mipihte` 'group members', all in clearly nominal contexts.

No rows from this nominal domain have been promoted as verbal applicative evidence. The homophony is real and the synchronic relationship between the two `-pih` forms remains an open question in the literature.

# Boundary with directionals, VP stacking, and `-pih` interactions

The form `paikhiatpih` 'bring out with' shows `-pih` stacked after the directional suffix `-khiat` 'away / out'. ZNC (2017/2018) also documents `pai-pih-suk` 'walk down along with', and Otsuka (2011) notes that `-pih` and the relinquitive `-san` can co-occur. These stacked forms are important for completeness but they remain boundary material in this first account.

Similarly, the high-frequency forms `hopih`, `hehpih`, and `ompih` appear extensively in the corpus in contexts that may involve benefactive-adjacent or stative-comitative readings. These have not been promoted here because the analysis remains unresolved and the individual frequencies do not convert directly into grammar-facing claims.

# Deferred and boundary material

Several areas remain outside this account.

- `paipih` and `tunpih` are the clearest promoted forms, but they do not exhaust the comitative applicative domain.
- `hopih`, `hehpih`, `ompih`, `ciahpih`, and `tenpih` appear frequently but their precise applicative vs lexicalized status remains unresolved.
- The Form II restriction is stated from literature; corpus verification of the restriction should remain explicit.
- Nominal `innkuanpihte` and `mipihte` remain boundary material and must not be promoted as verbal evidence.
- Stacked directional forms such as `paikhiatpih` remain boundary material pending VP-slot analysis.
- The potential benefactive reading of `-pih` (noted by Otsuka as a possible secondary sense) is not promoted and remains an open question.
- The comitative system as a whole — including `-khawm` 'together' and case-marked comitative strategies — lies outside the present narrow account.

# Summary

The safest grammar-facing conclusion is a narrow one: Tedim has a verbal `-pih` comitative applicative suffix that attaches to Form II / Stem 2 verbs and introduces a comitative participant or comitative object as a new core argument. The forms `paipih`, `nekpih`, and `tunpih` provide the clearest current evidence. Nominal `-pih` 'member / group member' must remain a boundary category, and stacked directional and VP forms remain outside the present core claim.
