# Editorial scope

These are short print-facing dictionary drafts for the interrogatives slice. They are aligned to `candidates_interrogatives.tsv`, interpreted through `dossier_interrogatives.md`, and cross-referenced to `grammar_interrogatives_print_slice.md`. They preserve candidate-backed examples and blocked false friends rather than turning the topic into a raw search over every `hiam` or `bang` form.

These entries are editorial review material only. They do not imply changes to analyzer dictionaries, machine dictionary files, or lexical source tables.

## hiam

Category: interrogative particle / sentence-final question particle

Gloss: `Q`; 'question particle'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Clause-final `hiam`"*

Status: draft-ready with caveat

`Hiam` marks questions in the current candidate-backed print packet. The safest current print claim is clause-final `hiam` in the accepted examples. At the same time, not every surface `hiam` string is print-safe. Lexical or non-interrogative sequences such as `a hiam ciat uh` and `langnih a hiam namsau` remain blocked, and formulaic `Bang hang hiam cih leh` is not an ordinary `hiam` question example.

(@dict:hiam-1)
a. Tedim: Na pa inn-ah kote giah nading a awng ding hiam
b. Segmentation: Na | pa' | inn-ah | kote' | giah | nading | a | awng | ding | hiam
c. Gloss: 2SG | male.POSS | house-LOC | 1PL.PRO.POSS | camp | PURP | 3SG | open | IRR | Q
d. Translation: 'Is there room in thy father's house for us to lodge?'

Editorial note: this attested Genesis 24:23 clause is the main print anchor. The older report paraphrase `Inn-ah hong tum theih na hiam` should not be promoted as the dictionary example.

## bang

Category: interrogative word

Gloss: 'what'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "WH + `hiam` content questions"*

Status: draft-ready with analyzer caveat

`Bang` is represented in the current candidate layer by `Bang ahi hiam?` The analyzer glosses this `bang` as `like`, but the clause-level context supports the ordinary what-question reading. Bang-family forms such as `bangmah` and `bangin` are blocked false friends and should not be treated as ordinary `bang` interrogatives.

(@dict:bang-1)
a. Tedim: Bang ahi hiam?
b. Segmentation: Bang | ahi | hiam
c. Gloss: what | be.3SG | Q
d. Translation: 'What is it?'

## kua

Category: interrogative word

Gloss: 'who'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "WH + `hiam` content questions"*

Status: draft-ready with analyzer caveat

`Kua` is represented by `Hihte kua ahi hiam?` and also by `Topa longal Pasian kua hiam`. In the candidate rows the analyzer tags `kua` as `NUM`, but the surrounding clause-level evidence supports the interrogative who-reading. That caveat should stay visible in the entry.

(@dict:kua-1)
a. Tedim: Hihte kua ahi hiam?
b. Segmentation: Hihte | kua | ahi | hiam
c. Gloss: these | who | be.3SG | Q
d. Translation: 'Who are these?'

(@dict:kua-2)
a. Tedim: Topa longal Pasian kua hiam
b. Segmentation: Topa | longal | Pasian | kua | hiam
c. Gloss: Lord | apart.from | God | who | Q
d. Translation: 'Who is God save the LORD?'

## bangci

Category: interrogative word / manner question word

Gloss: 'how'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "WH + `hiam` content questions"*

Status: draft-ready

`Bangci` should remain visible as `bangci` and should not be flattened into a generic `bang` entry. Genesis 3:13 is the cleanest current candidate-backed `bangci` row, and it is the best print dictionary anchor for the manner question word.

(@dict:bangci-1)
a. Tedim: Bangci a hici gamtat na hi hiam?
b. Segmentation: Bangci | a | hi-ci | gam-tat | na | hi | hiam
c. Gloss: how | 3SG | this-say | conduct-do | 2SG | DECL | Q
d. Translation: 'How have you acted thus?'

## banghangin

Category: interrogative expression / reason question word

Gloss: 'why'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "WH + `hiam` content questions"*

Status: draft-ready with segmentation caveat

The current candidate row treats Genesis 4:6 as `banghangin` / reason-question evidence. The analyzer exports the sequence as `bang | hang-in`, and that segmentation caveat should remain visible in the entry. At the same time, the row should not be collapsed into a raw `bang` example, and formulaic `Bang hang hiam cih leh` should not be reused as an ordinary `banghangin` question example.

(@dict:banghangin-1)
a. Tedim: bang hangin na mai sia ahi hiam
b. Segmentation: bang | hang-in | na | mai | sia | ahi | hiam
c. Gloss: what | reason-ERG | 2SG | face | bad | be.3SG | Q
d. Translation: 'Why is thy countenance fallen?'

## Embedded-question note

`Bang hiam cih thei lo uh hi` remains visible as embedded-question material, but it is not yet promoted here as a full draft-ready dictionary entry or as an ordinary independent `hiam` question. It belongs to later work on embedded questions or complementation.

## Blocked and deferred material

The current slice keeps several controls explicit. `A hiam ciat uh` and `langnih a hiam namsau` are blocked lexical or non-interrogative `hiam` rows. `Bang hang hiam cih leh` is a formulaic explanatory frame, not an ordinary `hiam` or `banghangin` question. `Bangmah` and `bangin` are blocked bang-family false friends. Comparison particles `maw`, `ham`, and `em` remain deferred and should not yet be entered as part of this core `hiam` / WH dictionary slice.
