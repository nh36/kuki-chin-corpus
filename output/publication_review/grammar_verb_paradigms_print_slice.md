# Overview of basic finite verb paradigms in Tedim

This section gives a narrow first account of Tedim finite verb paradigms. It focuses on the safest finite predicate frames and person-marked finite predicates, then keeps adjacent domains explicit as boundaries.

The strongest starting anchors are `kanei` 'I have', `na si ding hi` '2SG die IRR DECL', `a suak hi` '3SG become DECL', and `a en uh hi` '3SG look.at PL DECL'. These support a practical finite-frame analysis without expanding into chapter-scale verbal morphology.

This section is not a complete verbal paradigm system, not a full TAM chapter, not a full negation chapter, not a full stem-alternation chapter, not a full agreement chapter, not a full object-prefix chapter, not a full transitivity chapter, and not a full voice chapter.

# Safest finite predicate frame

The most stable frame in this first slice is a person-marked predicate followed by clause-final `hi`. This frame is visible in both Old Testament and Gospel evidence.

(@ex:vp-frame-gen21) Genesis 21:7
a. Tedim: Ahi zongin a teek khitin amah tawh tapa khat ka nei hi.
b. Segmentation: ahi | zongin | a | teek | khitin | amah | tawh | tapa | khat | ka-nei | hi
c. Gloss: but | then | 3SG | old.age | after | 3SG.PRO | with | son | one | 1SG-have | DECL
d. Translation: Yet when she was old, I have a son by him. (Genesis 21:7)

Matthew 27:36 provides the Gospel comparandum for the same finite frame.

(@ex:vp-frame-matt27) Matthew 27:36
a. Tedim: Jesuh a en uh hi.
b. Segmentation: Jesuh | a | en | uh | hi
c. Gloss: Jesus | 3SG | look.at | 2/3PL | DECL
d. Translation: They watched Jesus. (Matthew 27:36)

Together these rows show a finite frame that is useful for grammar-facing description even before a complete paradigm grid is attempted.

# Safest person-marked finite predicates

The clearest person-marked finite examples currently available show second-, third-, and first-person subject-marking patterns in controlled clauses.

(@ex:vp-person-gen2) Genesis 2:17
a. Tedim: Bang hang hiam cih leh tua na nek ni-in na si ding hi.
b. Segmentation: bang | hang | hiam | cih | leh | tua | na | nek | ni-in | na | si | ding | hi
c. Gloss: what | reason | Q | say | if | that | 2SG | eat.II | day-LOC | 2SG | die | IRR | DECL
d. Translation: For in the day that you eat of it, you will surely die. (Genesis 2:17)

Genesis 26:13 adds a third-person finite predicate row.

(@ex:vp-person-gen26) Genesis 26:13
a. Tedim: a hau mahmah khat a suak hi.
b. Segmentation: a | hau | mahmah | khat | a | suak | hi
c. Gloss: 3SG | rich | very | one | 3SG | become | DECL
d. Translation: He became very rich. (Genesis 26:13)

John 4:17 supplies the Gospel first-person row.

(@ex:vp-person-john4) John 4:17
a. Tedim: Numei in, “Pasal ka nei kei hi,” a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: numei | in | pasal | ka-nei | kei | hi | a | ci | hi
c. Gloss: woman | ERG | husband | 1SG-have | NEG | DECL | 3SG | say | DECL
d. Translation: The woman said, “I have no husband.” (John 4:17)

These rows are enough for a narrow person-marked finite-predicate claim, while still leaving the larger person-prefix architecture to dedicated sections.

# A small set of paradigm-friendly verb anchors

For this first slice, the safest lexical anchors are `nei` 'have' and `suak` 'become', together with the finite-frame use of clause-final `hi` 'DECL'. This keeps the section finite-frame oriented instead of trying to normalize every verb class at once.

# Boundary with negation and TAM

Finite-frame evidence intersects negation and TAM quickly. Rows such as `ta nei lo hi` and `lutthei ding uh hi` remain visible, but they are treated as boundary material here so the established negation and TAM sections remain authoritative for those analyses.

# Boundary with stem alternation

The `mu / muh` 'see' family remains a key boundary. It is important for paradigms, but the Form I/Form II distribution and caveats are handled in the dedicated stem-alternation section.

# Boundary with object-prefix / inverse-like material

Participant-oriented object-prefix material remains outside the core finite table in this section. The person-sensitive prefix contrasts are already treated in the dedicated hong/kong section and stay boundary-controlled here.

# Boundary with analyzer-gap and report-only material

Analyzer-gap material and report-table artifacts remain blocked or deferred in this first slice. Inclusive report paradigms and conditioned negative-ability variants are kept visible as limits rather than promoted as settled grammar facts.

# Deferred material

Several issues remain outside the present account.

- Full person-prefix paradigms, including clusivity and plural routing, remain for dedicated sections.
- Full TAM and modal stacking remains outside this finite-frame slice.
- Full negation architecture remains outside this finite-frame slice.
- Full stem alternation, voice-like behavior, and object-prefix selection remain outside this finite-frame slice.
- Raw report counts are not used as grammar facts.

# Summary

The strongest defensible first claim is modest: Tedim has stable finite predicate frames and a small set of clear person-marked finite anchors that can already be described in grammar-facing prose. The section remains intentionally narrow and keeps TAM, negation, stem alternation, object-prefix, and analyzer-gap issues explicit as boundaries.
