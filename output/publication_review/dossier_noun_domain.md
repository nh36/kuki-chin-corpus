## Scope and status

This dossier now serves as the normalized interpretation layer for the noun domain section. It stands between the raw noun-domain reports and the publication-facing slice in `grammar_noun_domain_print_slice.md`, and it keeps the current pass aligned with the coverage-normalization standard from `coverage_normalization_audit.md`.

## Safe publication-facing claims

- Simple lexical nouns are now safely identifiable in the current packet. `gam` and `aksi` are strong common-noun anchors.
- Plural marking with `-te` is printable for controlled noun examples such as `aksi-te` and `mite`.
- Human noun `mi` is a stable lexical noun head rather than a report artifact. The same noun remains visible in larger phrases such as `mi khat`, `mi khempeuh`, `mi pawlkhat`, and `mi tampi`.
- Nonhuman nouns such as `ni` and `kum` also remain visible as noun heads in counted phrases.
- `minam` is safe enough for a cautious note about transparent compound-like nouns.

## Safe only with explicit caveats

- `aksi` is a useful Gospel simple-stem anchor in `ama aksi`, but that row occurs in a possessed phrase rather than in isolation.
- `gam` is safe as a lexical noun, but the exact semantic range between 'land', 'country', and broader territory readings varies by context.
- `minam` is print-usable with caveat, not a settled compound analysis for the whole lexicon.
- Proper-name material such as `Abraham` shows that names behave nominally, but current evidence does not support a full proper-noun system.
- `-te` is safe as a noun plural anchor in the checked rows, but the wider plurality system is still underdescribed.

## Deferred and boundary material

- broader compound classes and lexicalized compounds
- proper-name inventories and multiword Bible names
- classifier-like and measure-like nouns
- kinship nouns where the clearest data still overlap with possession
- nominalized nouns in `-na`
- plural and number patterns beyond straightforward `-te` marking
- analyzer-noisy noun tags and raw report-only noun lists

## Current editorial conclusion

The noun domain is now strong enough for a publication-facing section that is no longer limited to a thin lexical packet. The current normalized section can describe basic noun stems, cautious plural marking, human common nouns, and nouns as heads inside larger phrases. It should still stop short of claiming a full noun-domain chapter until compounds, proper nouns, nominalization boundaries, and broader plurality patterns receive the same level of controlled normalization.
