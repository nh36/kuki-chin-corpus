# Editorial scope

These entries are short print-facing dictionary drafts for the pronoun slice. They are meant to be readable in a grammar-and-dictionary workflow rather than as raw corpus notes. Each entry gives a category, a reader-facing gloss, an editorial status, a short explanation, and one or two examples.

## kei

Category: independent personal pronoun

Gloss: `1SG`; 'I, me'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Personal pronouns"*

Status: draft-ready

`kei` is the ordinary free first-person singular pronoun. It can appear as subject, object, or oblique complement, and it remains distinct from the bound prefix `ka-`.

(@dict:kei-1)
a. Tedim: Note' vun dawn ki-at ding a, tua pen kei le note' kikalah thuciamna lim khat hi ding hi.
b. Segmentation: kei
c. Gloss: 1SG.PRO
d. Translation: 'And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you.'

## nang

Category: independent personal pronoun

Gloss: `2SG`; 'you'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Personal pronouns"*

Status: draft-ready

`nang` is the ordinary free second-person singular pronoun. It is distinct from the bound prefix `na-`, though the two belong to the same person-marking system.

(@dict:nang-1)
a. Tedim: Na sanggampa' sisan a hup dingin a kam a ka leitang tungah tu-in nang hong kisamsiat hi.
b. Segmentation: nang
c. Gloss: 2SG.PRO
d. Translation: 'And now art thou cursed from the earth, which hath opened her mouth to receive thy brother's blood from thy hand;'

## amah

Category: independent personal pronoun

Gloss: `3SG`; 'he, she, him, her'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Personal pronouns"*

Status: draft-ready

`amah` is the free third-person singular pronoun. In discourse it often has more prominence than a bound third-person prefix, but it is safe to print it simply as the ordinary independent third-person form.

(@dict:amah-1)
a. Tedim: Mipa in a zi' min pen Eve, ci hi. Bang hang hiam cih leh amah pen mihing khempeuh' nu ahi hi.
b. Segmentation: amah
c. Gloss: 3SG.PRO
d. Translation: 'And Adam called his wife's name Eve; because she was the mother of all living.'

## amaute

Category: independent personal pronoun

Gloss: `3PL`; 'they, them'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Personal pronouns"*

Status: draft-ready

`amaute` is the ordinary free third-person plural pronoun. It is worth including because it rounds out the print-facing personal-pronoun paradigm without requiring a separate agreement chapter.

(@dict:amaute-1)
a. Tedim: Topa Pasian in mipa le a zi a dingin savun puan bawl a, amaute silhsak hi.
b. Segmentation: amaute
c. Gloss: 3PL.PRO
d. Translation: 'Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them.'

## note

Category: independent personal pronoun

Gloss: `2PL`; 'you (plural)'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Personal pronouns"*

Status: draft-ready

`note` is the ordinary free second-person plural pronoun. It also has a possessive use in forms such as `note'`, but the free plural pronoun is the clearest print entry.

(@dict:note-1)
a. Tedim: En un, note le note' khit a na suanlekhakte uh le,
b. Segmentation: note
c. Gloss: 2PL.PRO
d. Translation: 'And I, behold, I establish my covenant with you, and with your seed after you;'

## eite

Category: independent first-person plural pronoun

Gloss: `1PL`; 'we, us' (clear inclusive uses; exact clusivity under review)

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Inclusive and exclusive first-person plural"*

Status: analytically provisional

`eite` is a first-person plural pronoun with clear inclusive uses in the Bible corpus, including contexts where the addressee is explicitly part of the group. The same dossier evidence also shows that `eite/ei` is not yet cleanly settled as a globally inclusive series in every sampled context, so the entry should stay provisional rather than forcing a single clusivity label.

(@dict:eite-1)
a. Tedim: Tua ciangin Abram in Lot' kiangah, "Nang le kei' kikal, nang' gancingte le kei' gancingte' kikalah kitotna omsak kei ni. Bang hang hiam cih leh eite beh khat ihi hi."
b. Segmentation: eite
c. Gloss: 1PL.PRO
d. Translation: 'And Abram said unto Lot, Let there be no strife, I pray thee, between me and thee, and between my herdmen and thy herdmen; for we be brethren.'

## kote

Category: independent first-person plural pronoun

Gloss: `1PL.EXCL`; 'we, us' (hearer excluded)

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Inclusive and exclusive first-person plural"*

Status: draft-ready as exclusive

The clusivity dossier gives strong dialogue-context evidence that `kote` is an exclusive first-person plural form. This entry can therefore be printed as `1PL.EXCL`, even though the wider `ei/eite` series still needs review before the full Tedim plural system is treated as closed.

(@dict:kote-1)
a. Tedim: Kote tawh kitenna hong bawl un. Na tanute uh kote' tungah hong pia unla, no a dingin ka tanute uh la un.
b. Segmentation: kote
c. Gloss: 1PL.PRO
d. Translation: 'And make ye marriages with us, and give your daughters unto us, and take our daughters unto you.'

## ka-

Category: pronominal and possessive prefix

Gloss: `1SG.POSS`; also 1st-person concord before verbs

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Possessive prefixes"*

Status: draft-ready

Before nouns, `ka-` is the ordinary first-person singular possessive prefix and is safely glossed 'my'. In wider clause structure it also participates in first-person pronominal concord, including the exclusive plural in Henderson's account.

(@dict:ka-prefix-1)
a. Tedim: Pasian in ka pa' inn panin hong paikhiatsak
b. Segmentation: ka pa' inn
c. Gloss: 1SG.POSS father house
d. Translation: 'from my father's house'

## na-

Category: pronominal and possessive prefix

Gloss: `2SG.POSS`; also 2nd-person concord before verbs

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Possessive prefixes"*

Status: draft-ready

Before nouns, `na-` is the ordinary second-person possessive prefix. The same segmental form also belongs to the verbal person-marking system, so print dictionary prose should note both functions without collapsing them.

(@dict:na-prefix-1)
a. Tedim: Kua' tanu na hi hiam, hong gen in. Na pa' inn-ah kote' giah nading a awng ding hiam? a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: na pa' inn-ah
c. Gloss: 2SG.POSS father house-LOC
d. Translation: 'Whose daughter art thou? tell me, I pray thee: is there room in thy father's house for us to lodge in?'

## a-

Category: pronominal and possessive prefix

Gloss: `3SG.POSS`; also default third-person concord

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Possessive prefixes"*

Status: draft-ready

`a-` is the ordinary third-person possessive prefix before nouns and the default third-person concord marker in wider clause structure. Because it is so frequent and multifunctional, the print entry should be short and conservative.

(@dict:a-prefix-1)
a. Tedim: Mipa in a zi' min pen Eve, ci hi.
b. Segmentation: a zi'
c. Gloss: 3SG.POSS wife
d. Translation: 'And the man called his wife's name Eve.'

## ki-

Category: reflexive and reciprocal verbal prefix

Gloss: `REFL`; 'self-, reciprocally, together'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Reflexive and reciprocal marking with `ki-`"*

Status: draft-ready

`ki-` is a productive verbal prefix used in reflexive and reciprocal predicates. Some surface `ki-` forms are lexicalized, so the entry should stay with transparent verbal examples.

(@dict:ki-prefix-1)
a. Tedim: Tua thu hangin pasal in a nu le a pa nusia-in a zi tawh kigawm a, amau tegel pum khat a bang uh hi.
b. Segmentation: ki-gawm
c. Gloss: REFL-join
d. Translation: 'Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.'

## -mah

Category: emphatic suffix

Gloss: `EMPH`; 'self, in person, emphatically'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Emphatic pronouns in `-mah`"*

Status: draft-ready

The suffix `-mah` derives emphatic pronouns such as `keimah` and `nangmah`. It also appears in forms like `kuamah` and `bangmah`, where it contributes to a negative-indefinite series, but the core print entry should begin from the emphatic-pronoun use.

(@dict:mah-1)
a. Tedim: Abimelek in, En in, ka leitang, na mai-ah om hi. Nangmah in hoih na sakna munah teng in, a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: nang-mah
c. Gloss: 2SG-EMPH
d. Translation: 'And Abimelech said, Behold, my land is before thee: dwell where it pleaseth thee.'

## hong-

Category: participant-oriented preverbal prefix

Gloss: 'toward speaker/addressee; often with 3rd-person acting on a speech-act participant'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Pronominal prefixes and inverse/directional marking"*

Status: analytically provisional

`hong-` belongs to the person-sensitive preverbal system, but its exact range still needs careful review because not every surface `hong` is the same prefix. The print slice therefore keeps the entry narrow and uses a manually checked example rather than an automatic one.

(@dict:hong-prefix-1)
a. Tedim: Amah in a sangna panin a khut tawh zamin kei hong la a, tui thukpi panin keimah hong kaikhia hi.
b. Segmentation: hong la
c. Gloss: 3>1 take
d. Translation: 'He sent from above, he took me, He drew me out of many waters.'

## kong-

Category: participant-oriented preverbal prefix

Gloss: 'toward you; often with 1st-person acting on a 2nd-person participant'

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Pronominal prefixes and inverse/directional marking"*

Status: analytically provisional

`kong-` is clearer in context than many current `hong-` examples, but it still belongs to the same not-yet-fully-settled person-sensitive prefix system. It is best printed as a cautious participant-oriented form rather than as a fully closed paradigm label.

(@dict:kong-prefix-1)
a. Tedim: Faro in Josef' kiangah, Egypt gam khempeuh a uk dingin nang kong koih khinzo hi, a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: kong koih
c. Gloss: 1>2 set
d. Translation: 'And Pharaoh said unto Joseph, See, I have set thee over all the land of Egypt.'
