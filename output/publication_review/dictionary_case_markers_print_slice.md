# Editorial scope

These entries are short print-facing dictionary drafts for the case-marking slice. They are meant to be readable in a grammar-and-dictionary workflow rather than as raw corpus notes. Each entry gives a category, a reader-facing gloss, an editorial status, a short explanation, and one or two examples.

The current entries are aligned to `candidates_case_marking.tsv` and interpreted against `dossier_case_marking.md`. They therefore preserve candidate-backed anchors, blocked rows, and structural caveats rather than trying to flatten the packet into a bare suffix list.

## -ah

Category: locative case marker / postpositional suffix

Gloss: `LOC`; ‘at, in, on, to’

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Locative `-ah`”*

Status: draft-ready

The suffix `-ah` marks location and, in many contexts, destination. The current packet distinguishes simple noun-plus-locative evidence such as `khua-ah` from relator-noun examples such as `laizangah` and `vantungah`, so `-ah` should not be treated only as a bare place suffix.

(@dict:ah-1)
a. Tedim: leitung khua a vaksak dingin vantungah khuavak hi uh hen,” ci hi.
b. Segmentation: vantung-ah
c. Gloss: heaven-LOC
d. Translation: ‘and let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth’

(@dict:ah-2)
a. Tedim: Tuite' laizangah van kuumpi om hen la, tua van kuumpi in tui le tui kikhensak hen.
b. Segmentation: lai-zang-ah
c. Gloss: middle-side-LOC
d. Translation: ‘Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters.’

The second example is important because it shows `-ah` after a relational stem rather than after a simple place noun. The candidate layer keeps that subtype separate from the plain locative control `khua-ah`, and the analyzer's occasional `FUNC` label on these bases is treated as an export caveat rather than as the final grammatical analysis.

## -in

Category: ergative case marker

Gloss: `ERG`

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Ergative `-in`”*

Status: draft-ready with manually selected example

The marker `-in` marks the transitive subject. The current candidate-backed print anchor is `Kain in` in Genesis 4:3. Many other corpus rows for `-in` are complicated by homographic or non-case material, so this entry keeps the accepted anchor and blocks rows such as `ciangin` from being treated as ergative examples.

(@dict:in-1)
a. Tedim: Tua hun sungin Kain in lei pana piang gah piakna Topa' tungah paipih a,
b. Segmentation: Kain in
c. Gloss: Cain ERG
d. Translation: ‘And in process of time it came to pass, that Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD.’

Editorial note: the grammatical analysis is secure, but broader `-in` promotion is still blocked pending better discrimination between nominal and verbal or conjunctional `-in` rows.

## -pan

Category: ablative / source marker

Gloss: `ABL`; ‘from, out of’

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Source marking: `-pan` and `-panin`”*

Status: draft-ready

The suffix `-pan` marks source, origin, or point of departure. The current candidate-backed example is `lakpan`, so the entry should be read as source marking on a relator noun rather than as a bare suffix floating free of spatial nominal structure.

(@dict:pan-1)
a. Tedim: hih thukhamte lakpan a neupente khat bek nangawn zuikha loin
b. Segmentation: lak-pan
c. Gloss: midst-ABL
d. Translation: ‘from among these commandments, even the least one’

This example shows clearly that `-pan` can mark source on a relational noun phrase, not only on a simple place expression.

## -panin

Category: source-marking form related to `-pan`

Gloss: ‘from; from out of; source-marking form related to `-pan`’

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Source marking: `-pan` and `-panin`”*

Status: needs review

The form `-panin` is frequent and clearly expresses source, but its precise structural analysis still needs review. In some contexts it behaves like an extended source form closely related to `-pan`; in others it appears in tighter or more fused patterns that deserve separate editorial checking. The analyzer segmentation `pan-in` is useful evidence, but the structural analysis remains under review.

(@dict:panin-1)
a. Tedim: Na gam, na beh, le na pa' inn panin nang kong lah ding gamah pai in.
b. Segmentation: inn panin
c. Gloss: house from
d. Translation: ‘Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father’s house.’

Editorial note: the uncertainty belongs in the analysis, not in the headline gloss. The entry should therefore read as a source-marking form first, with structural cautions reserved for the prose note.

## -tawh

Category: comitative / associative postposition

Gloss: `COM`; ‘with, together with’; by extension also ‘with, by means of’

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Comitative `-tawh`”*

Status: draft-ready

The core use of `-tawh` is comitative or associative: it marks a companion or accompanying participant. In the biblical corpus it also extends into material and instrument-like meanings, so both uses should be represented honestly in the entry and kept separate in the same way they are kept separate in the candidate layer.

(@dict:tawh-1)
a. Tedim: Tangvalte' neksate le kei tawh hong kuan mite' tanh dingte lobuang bangmah ka la kei ding hi.
b. Segmentation: kei tawh
c. Gloss: 1SG COM
d. Translation: ‘save only that which the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men which went with me’

(@dict:tawh-2)
a. Tedim: Topa Pasian in leilak pana leivui tawh mihing bawl a
b. Segmentation: leivui tawh
c. Gloss: dust COM
d. Translation: ‘And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground’

The first example illustrates accompaniment. The second illustrates a broader material or means reading. The entry should therefore keep accompaniment and material/instrumental extension distinct rather than collapsing both under a vague English *with*.

## Relator nouns in the current packet

The current packet treats `kiang`, `sung`, `tung`, `lak`, `laizang`, and `vantung` as relational stems that host case or postpositional marking. They are not merely accidental examples parked under suffix entries. This is why the `-ah` and `-pan` entries keep relator-noun constructions visible instead of flattening them into a suffix-only inventory.

## kiang

Category: relator noun

Gloss: ‘beside, near, in the presence of’

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Relator nouns”*

Status: draft-ready

The noun `kiang` is a relational stem meaning ‘beside, near, at someone’s side’. It frequently combines with locative and source marking, especially in forms such as `kiangah` and `kiang panin`, and it should be read as part of the same case/postposition packet rather than as an incidental lexical example.

(@dict:kiang-1)
a. Tedim: nang na hih leh, nuamtakin na pu na pate kiang na zuan ding a
b. Segmentation: kiang
c. Gloss: beside
d. Translation: ‘thou shalt go to thy fathers in peace’

## sung

Category: relator noun

Gloss: ‘inside, within, among’

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, “Relator nouns”*

Status: draft-ready

The noun `sung` is one of the most productive relational stems in the corpus. It combines freely with locative and source marking and is central to the way Tedim builds spatial expressions.

(@dict:sung-1)
a. Tedim: Seth a suah khit ciangin Adam kum zagiat sung nungta lai hi.
b. Segmentation: sung
c. Gloss: inside
d. Translation: ‘And the days of Adam after he had begotten Seth were eight hundred years.’
