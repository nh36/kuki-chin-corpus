# 1. What the dedicated report claims

The dedicated relative-clause report (`docs/grammar/reports/08-clause-03-relatives.md`) claims a broad relative-clause domain centered on `a-` prefixed clauses plus nominalized and pluralized forms. For this packet, the evidence is split more narrowly:

- **Clear `mi`-headed relative-like evidence**: `a bawl mi` with a source-resolved Bible example.
- **`mi`-headed or `mi`-nominalized relative-like inventory**: report-backed patterns such as `a nei mi`, but not yet source-resolved in this packet.
- **Possible headed non-`mi` rows**: `a om lai`, `a gen thu` appear in report tables but remain unresolved at verse level here.
- **Relative-like `a-` material**: structurally plausible, but `a-` also overlaps ordinary 3SG agreement.
- **Nominalized `-na` material**: forms such as `omna` are real and source-resolved, but overlap the nominalization domain.
- **Case-marked nominalized clauses**: forms such as `muhna-ah` are source-resolved but align more strongly with nominalization + case.
- **Ordinary subordination and clause-linkage material**: `ciangin`, `dingin`, `bawlin`, `semin`, and support-only `ahih ciangin` remain boundaries for this packet.
- **Report-table or generated rows**: useful orientation (`a om lai`, `a gen thu`) but not promotable until source-resolved.

# 2. Safest print-facing label

The concrete print-facing decision is:

**`mi`-headed and nominalized relative-like constructions**

This is safer than a broad `relative clauses` label because current source-resolved evidence is strongest for `mi`-headed material, while `-na` and case-marked forms must remain explicitly separated from core relative diagnostics.

# 4. Example quality and source resolution

Promoted and supporting examples are source-resolved and constructionally aligned:

- `a bawl mi` (Exodus 30:38): clean `a-VERB + mi` span, identifiable head, and direct translation support.
- `omte` (Genesis 2:1): source-resolved support with transparent segmentation, retained as support because headless vs plural nominalization analysis remains open.

Rows such as `a om lai` and `a gen thu` remain unresolved because this packet does not yet attach source-resolved Bible lines for formal promotion.

# 5. Boundary controls

## 5.1 Relationship to nominalization

`omna` and other `-na` forms remain nominalization boundary material unless a tighter relative analysis is demonstrated.

## 5.2 Relationship to case-marked nominalization

`muhna-ah` remains case-marked nominalization boundary material, not a promoted relative-clause diagnostic.

## 5.3 Relationship to NP structure and noun domain

The packet records head/head-like behavior, but does not settle whether `mi` is best analyzed as head noun, nominalizer, relativizer, or a mixed constructional role across all tokens.

## 5.4 Relationship to clause linkage

Ordinary `ciangin`, purposive `dingin`, same-subject `bawlin`/`semin`, and support-only `ahih ciangin` remain outside the relative-clause core for this packet.

# 6. Diagnostic conclusion

- **Print-facing label**: `mi`-headed and nominalized relative-like constructions.
- **True first-slice diagnostic**: `a bawl mi` (promoted).
- **Relative-like support**: `omte` (supporting).
- **Nominalization boundaries**: `omna` (boundary), `muhna-ah` (case-marked nominalization boundary).
- **Ordinary subordination boundaries**: `ciangin`, `dingin`.
- **Clause-linkage boundaries**: `bawlin`, `semin`, `ahih ciangin`.
- **Deferred/report-only rows**: `a om lai`, `a gen thu` pending source resolution.
- **Safe current claim**: the section can describe a narrow `mi`-headed and nominalized relative-like domain without claiming a full relative-clause system, full nominalization system, full NP structure system, full case system, full subordination chapter, or full discourse system.
