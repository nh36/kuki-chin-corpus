# Remaining unpacketized or blocked domains

The remaining gaps are no longer obvious “missing packet” domains of the same kind as transitivity.

Phonology/tone remains the clearest unpacketized major domain, but it is still blocked or theory-heavy rather than ready for a first packet. `docs/grammar/GRAMMAR_SOURCE_INVENTORY.md` and `docs/SKELETON_GRAMMAR.md` both retain the phonology/tone chapter expectation, yet `output/publication_review/whole_grammar_coverage_audit.md` still treats tone in `-a` and related analyzer-gap problems as unresolved enough to block a clean candidate-first packet.

Verb paradigms remain report-backed but unpacketized through `docs/grammar/reports/05-verb-00-paradigm-tables.md`. However, they are not currently comparable to transitivity as a first packet target: the audit and source map do not present paradigms as a clean dedicated topic layer with a narrow print-safe claim, and the paradigm tables currently function more as support for stem alternation, TAM, and transitivity than as a ready packet of their own.

Discourse beyond the current sentence-final particle slice also remains only partly surfaced. `docs/grammar/grammar_source_map.json` maps only narrow sentence-final topics such as declarative `hi` and topic marker `pen`, while broader discourse material still overlaps clause linkage, sentence-final particles, and generated-summary territory. That leaves discourse broader than the current packet, but not yet as a clean first-pass packet comparable to transitivity.

Analyzer-gap topics remain cross-cutting blockers rather than candidate-first packet domains. The source inventory and analyzer-gap audit still keep tone in `-a` blocked, conditioned variants open, and several older `hong-`, `-sak`, `-pih`, and `-thei/-theih` issues only partly reconciled. Those are reasons to stabilize packets, not reasons to open a new grammar-facing packet immediately.

So the remaining unpacketized domains are either blocked/theory-heavy, report-only in a table-driven way, or too cross-cutting and boundary-heavy to outrank stabilization.

# Decision

There is no remaining report-backed, non-blocked domain clearly comparable to transitivity that still needs a first candidate/scoping packet.

Phonology/tone remains blocked or theory-heavy. Verb paradigms remain report-backed but not yet packet-shaped. Broader discourse and analyzer-gap topics remain partially surfaced or cross-cutting. The current narrow packets therefore should not be expanded before human review, and no second-pass packet currently outranks stabilization.

The correct decision is to hold the current publication-review packet set stable for human review rather than opening another new packet or forcing a second-pass expansion immediately.

# Recommended next agent task

The next agent task should be a stabilization/handoff step rather than another grammar packet.

Concretely, the next task should be to create or update a human-review handoff document that lists all review-note-stage packets, identifies which packets are deliberately narrow and should stay frozen pending review, and records the remaining blocked or second-pass domains such as phonology/tone, verb paradigms, broader discourse, analyzer-gap topics, `-pih`, hong-/kong-, switch reference, relative clauses, transparent compounds, and wider reduplication.
