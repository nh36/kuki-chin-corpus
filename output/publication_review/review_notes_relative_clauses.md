# Review notes for relative-clause packet

This document records questions for human review of the relative-clause packet. The packet is now at a first grammar-facing slice maturity level and is ready for editorial checking before further expansion.

## Overall scope question

1. Is **"`mi`-headed and nominalized relative-like constructions"** the right print-facing label, or should the grammar slice use **"Relative and nominalized relative-like clause modifiers"** (less hedged)? Or another title?

## Core diagnostic: `a bawl mi`

2. Does `a bawl mi` 'person who makes' truly work as a relative-clause formation in the Exodus 30:38 example (`paknamtui a bawl mi peuhmah` 'whoever makes any like it')? Is the head role correctly identified as patient/affected?

3. Is the `a-` prefix best interpreted as a **relativizer** in this context, or is it simply the **3SG agreement prefix** that happens to appear in a relative-like structure? The packet currently treats it as ambiguous; is that the right caution?

4. The report cites 22 tokens of `a bawl mi`. Are all of those tokens actually relative constructions, or do some belong to other grammatical categories (e.g., nominalization, complement clauses)?

## Supporting diagnostics: `omte` and `a om lai`

5. For `omte` 'those who exist' (Genesis 2:1), is the interpretation a **headless/free relative** or a **plural nominalization**? Both readings seem plausible. Should the packet present both interpretations, or should one be preferred?

6. The report cites 73 tokens of `omte`. Should the grammar slice include this as a supporting diagnostic, or does the headless/free relative ambiguity make it too unclear?

7. For `a om lai` 'place where (it) exists', the report cites 133 tokens. Can a specific Bible verse be identified for source resolution? If so, should this pattern be included as a second promoted or supporting diagnostic alongside `a bawl mi`?

## Boundary material: Nominalization and case

8. Are `omna` 'place of being' and `muhna-ah` 'in (someone's) sight' correctly kept as **boundary material** (not promoted to the relative-clause core)? Or should one or both be promoted despite the nominalization and case-marking overlap?

9. The nominalization section treats `-na` as a productive nominalizer and keeps `omna` as nominalization boundary material. Does the relative-clause packet correctly defer to the nominalization section for these forms?

10. The form `muhna-ah` combines nominalization with locative case marking. Is this construction best understood as nominalization + case marking (as the packet suggests), or is it primarily a relative-clause formation that happens to take case marking?

## Cross-section relationships

11. The prefix/agreement section uses `a-` as a 3SG agreement marker. Does the relative-clause packet correctly preserve the ambiguity between relativizer and agreement? Should the prefix section be consulted about `a-` behavior in relative-like contexts?

12. The nominalization section treats `-na` as a productive deverbal nominalizer. Does the relative-clause packet create any conflicts with the nominalization account by treating `-na` forms as relative-clause boundary material?

13. The switch-reference packet already classifies `a bawl mi` (Exodus 30:38) and `omna` (Genesis 4:16) as boundary material. Are those classifications still appropriate given the new relative-clause packet, or should they be re-classified?

## Corpus and frequency questions

14. The report cites form counts (e.g., 22 tokens of `a bawl mi`, 133 tokens of `a om lai`, 73 tokens of `omte`). Are these counts accurate? Do they reflect only relative-clause uses, or do they include non-relative uses of the same form?

15. Should the packet expand to include report-backed patterns like `a nei mi` 'person who has' (63 tokens) and `a gen thu` 'word/matter that is said' (47 tokens)? What would be needed for source resolution?

## Print display questions

16. The grammar slice uses one formal interlinear example (`a bawl mi` from Exodus 30:38) and one supporting example (`omte` from Genesis 2:1). Is that enough evidence for the current slice, or should more examples be included?

17. Should the inventory table in the grammar slice include report-cited counts (e.g., "22 tokens of `a bawl mi`")? Does that add useful information or just clutter?

18. The deferred material section mentions `a om lai` and `a gen thu` as pending source resolution. Should the grammar slice include these as table rows with a "TBD" source note, or leave them completely out until a specific Bible verse is found?

## Structural clarifications

19. Does the packet correctly distinguish between:
    - Clear headed relatives (`a bawl mi` with `mi` as head)
    - Headless/free relatives (`omte`)
    - Nominalized relative-like forms (`omna`)
    - Case-marked nominalization (`muhna-ah`)?

20. Is the boundary control between relatives, nominalization, case marking, and subordination (clause linkage) clear enough to prevent overclaiming into those sections?
