# Overview of prefix and agreement routing in this section

This section treats a controlled routing contrast in Tedim pronominal prefixes. The forms `kanei` 'I have' and `kainn` 'my house' show that the same prefixal material can be analyzed as agreement before a verbal host and as possession before a nominal host. The segmented forms `ka-nei` '1SG-have' and `ka-inn` '1SG.POSS-house' make that host-sensitive contrast explicit.

# Agreement before verbal hosts

The prefix `ka-` 'first-person prefix' is treated as agreement when it attaches to a verbal host. In this controlled contrast, `kanei` 'I have' is the clearest anchor, and the segmentation `ka-nei` '1SG-have' expresses the agreement-side analysis.

(@ex:pref-kanei-gen21) Genesis 21:7
a. Tedim: Ahi zongin a teek khitin amah tawh tapa khat ka nei hi.
b. Segmentation: ahi | zongin | a | teek | khitin | amah | tawh | tapa | khat | ka-nei | hi
c. Gloss: but | then | 3SG | old.age | after | 3SG.PRO | with | son | one | 1SG-have | DECL
d. Translation: Yet when she was old, I have a son by him. (Genesis 21:7)

A Gospel counterpart appears in John 4:17.

(@ex:pref-kanei-john4) John 4:17
a. Tedim: Numei in, “Pasal ka nei kei hi,” a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: numei | in | pasal | ka-nei | kei | hi | a | ci | hi
c. Gloss: woman | ERG | husband | 1SG-have | NEG | DECL | 3SG | say | DECL
d. Translation: The woman said, “I have no husband.” (John 4:17)

# Possession before nominal hosts

Before a nominal host, the same prefixal material is analyzed as possession. The form `kainn` 'my house' and segmentation `ka-inn` '1SG.POSS-house' provide the clearest possessive-routing anchor.

(@ex:pref-kainn-gen15) Genesis 15:3
a. Tedim: Ta nong pia kei a, ka inn sunga a suak sila in keima gamh a luah ding hi ta hi.
b. Segmentation: ta | nong | pia | kei | a | ka-inn | sung-a | a | suak | sila | in | keima | gamh | a | luah | ding | hi
c. Gloss: child | 2SG | give | NEG | and | 1SG.POSS-house | inside-LOC | 3SG | servant | male | ERG | 1SG.PRO | inheritance | 3SG | take | IRR | DECL
d. Translation: You gave me no child, and a male servant from my house will inherit me. (Genesis 15:3)

A Gospel counterpart appears in Luke 7:6.

(@ex:pref-kainn-luke7) Luke 7:6
a. Tedim: Topa aw, ka inn-ah nong pai nadingin kei kilawm zo lo ka hih manin hong pai dah hoh in.
b. Segmentation: Topa | aw | ka-inn-ah | nong | pai | nadingin | kei | kilawm | zo | lo | ka | hih | manin
c. Gloss: Lord | VOC | 1SG.POSS-house-LOC | 2SG | come | for | 1SG.PRO | worthy | COMPL | NEG | 1SG | be | because
d. Translation: Lord, I am not worthy for you to come under my roof. (Luke 7:6)

# Agreement versus possession routing

Agreement versus possession routing is therefore host-sensitive. Verbal-host `ka-nei` supports agreement routing, while nominal-host `ka-inn` supports possessive routing. This controlled contrast does not yet settle the whole prefixal person system.

# Boundary with independent pronouns and clusivity

Independent pronoun and clusivity material remains secondary here. The form `ipai` 'we go / inclusive go' is kept as boundary material, because clusivity contrasts are handled primarily in the pronoun section rather than in this host-routing contrast.

# Boundary with relative clauses and a- material

The form `ainn` 'his/her house' and prefix `a-` 'third-person prefix' are important boundary material because they overlap possession, agreement, and relative-clause analysis. Relative-clause forms such as `a bawl mi` 'one who made' and clause-linkage material with `ahih` 'it is / which is' confirm that the `a-` family cannot yet be reduced to one finished account.

(@ex:pref-abawlmi-exod30) Exodus 30:38
a. Tedim: Tua tawh a kibangin paknamtui a bawl mi peuhmah ama mite' kiang panin kikhenkhia ding hi.
b. Segmentation: tua | tawh | a | kibangin | paknamtui | a | bawl | mi | peuhmah | ama | mite' | kiang | panin | kikhenkhia | ding | hi
c. Gloss: that | with | 3SG | like | perfume | REL | make | person | every | 3SG.PRO | people.POSS | side | from | separate | IRR | DECL
d. Translation: Whoever makes perfume like it shall be cut off from his people. (Exodus 30:38)

A Gospel counterpart appears in Matthew 13:41.

(@ex:pref-abawlmi-matt13) Matthew 13:41
a. Tedim: Mihing Tapa in a vantung mite hong sawl ding a, ama ukna sungah a om migilote le mi a mawh nadingin a bawl mi khempeuh kaikhawmin.
b. Segmentation: mihing | Tapa | in | a | vantung | mite | hong | sawl | ding | a | ama | ukna | sung-ah | a | om | migilote | le | mi | a | mawh | nadingin | a | bawl | mi | khempeuh | kaikhawm-in
c. Gloss: human | Son | ERG | 3SG | heaven | angels | VEN | send | IRR | and | 3SG.PRO | kingdom | inside-LOC | REL | be | evildoers | and | person | REL | sin | for | REL | make | person | all | gather-CVB
d. Translation: The Son of Man will send his angels, and they will gather all who cause sin. (Matthew 13:41)

# Boundary with object-prefix or inverse-like material

Object-prefix or inverse-like material is kept as boundary evidence. `hongmu` 'see me / come-see' and `kongmu` 'I see you / 1SG-see.2' remain visible because they are part of the same prefixal domain, but they are not promoted here to a full object-prefix or inverse chapter.

(@ex:pref-kongmu-jer7) Jeremiah 7:11
a. Tedim: Keimah in note' hih khempeuh kong mu khin hi, Topa in kong ci hi.
b. Segmentation: keimah | in | note' | hih | khempeuh | kong-mu | khin | hi | Topa | in | kong | ci | hi

d. Translation: I have seen all these things, says the Lord. (Jeremiah 7:11)

A Gospel counterpart appears in Matthew 25:37.

# Boundary with ki-

The form `kipan` 'from / begin / REFLEXIVE-boundary' remains boundary-only material. It overlaps reflexive/middle analysis and wider derivational behavior, so it is not treated here as a settled agreement prefix.

(@ex:pref-kipan-gen4) Genesis 4:26
a. Tedim: Tua hun a kipanin mite in Topa bia-in a min lawh ding kipan ta uh hi.
b. Segmentation: tua | hun | a | ki-pan-in | mite | in | Topa | bia-in | a | min | lawh | ding | ki-pan | ta | uh | hi
c. Gloss: that | time | 3SG | REFL-begin-ERG | people | ERG | LORD | worship-CVB | 3SG | name | call | IRR | REFL-begin | PFV | 2/3PL | DECL
d. Translation: From that time people began to call on the name of the Lord. (Genesis 4:26)

A Gospel counterpart appears in Matthew 4:17.

(@ex:pref-kipan-matt4) Matthew 4:17
a. Tedim: Tua hun a kipan Jesuh in thuhilh ding kipan a.
b. Segmentation: tua | hun | a | ki-pan | Jesuh | in | thu-hilh | ding | ki-pan | a
c. Gloss: that | time | 3SG | REFL-begin | Jesus | ERG | word-teach | IRR | REFL-begin | DECL
d. Translation: From that time Jesus began to preach. (Matthew 4:17)

# Boundary with apostrophe possession

Apostrophe possession is also treated as boundary material. Forms such as `Topa' inn` 'the Lord's house' belong primarily with the broader possession discussion and are not reopened here as a full possession analysis.

# Deferred and boundary material

Several issues remain outside the present account.

- `ka-nei` and `ka-inn` support a controlled host-sensitive routing contrast, but they do not settle the full person-prefix paradigm.
- `ainn` and the wider `a-` family remain boundary material shared with possession, agreement, and relative-clause structure.
- `ipai`, `ko`, and `ei` remain boundary material shared with independent pronoun and clusivity analysis.
- `hongmu` and `kongmu` remain boundary material until object-prefix and inverse-like selection conditions are treated directly.
- `kipan` remains boundary material shared with reflexive, middle, and derivational analysis.
- `Topa' inn` remains boundary material shared with apostrophe possession and possessor syntax.
- Raw occurrence counts are not treated as grammar facts without constructional control.

# Summary

The safest grammar-facing conclusion is a controlled routing contrast: verbal-host `ka-nei` supports agreement routing, and nominal-host `ka-inn` supports possessive routing. Boundary forms such as `ainn`, `ipai`, `hongmu`, `kongmu`, `kipan`, `a bawl mi`, `ahih`, and `Topa' inn` remain explicit so the section stays narrow and constructionally controlled.
