# Candidate groups

The current candidate layer is intentionally small and divided into a few reduplication groups.

## Productive-looking full reduplication

The clearest productive-looking rows are `mahmah`, `taktak`, and `peuhpeuh`.

`mahmah` is the safest current anchor because the report supports it repeatedly as a single-word full reduplication form, the analyzer recognizes it, and the report gives a worked example in `pha mahmah hi`. `taktak` is the best support row because it stays close to the same intensifying profile without forcing the packet to generalize from only one form.

`peuhpeuh` is also strong evidence for full reduplication, but it is slightly less clean for the first slice because it behaves more like a distributive or noun-modifying quantifier than the intensifying rows do.

## Syntactic reduplication

`ni ni` is the clearest syntactic reduplication row because the report explicitly contrasts the separate-token X X pattern with single-word morphological reduplication.

This makes it useful for scoping, but it is not the safest first print-facing claim because temporal or adverbial syntactic repetition also overlaps with VP-structure and TAM-facing interpretation.

## Verbal and aspectual-looking reduplication

`gengen` is the main verbal reduplication candidate in the current packet. It is useful because it shows that the report does not limit reduplication to intensifiers or quantifier-like forms.

`leuleu` is the clearest aspectual-looking row. The report treats it as iterative or continuative and even classifies it as temporal or aspectual. That makes it important boundary material, but it also means its main editorial pressure belongs partly with `output/publication_review/review_notes_tam.md` and `output/publication_review/review_notes_vp_structure_stacking.md`.

## Lexicalized or reduplicative-looking boundary rows

`kawikawi` is the clearest lexicalized or expressive-looking boundary row in the packet. It is explicit in the report, but it behaves more like spatial-totality or expressive adverbial material than a clean first reduplication anchor.

`theithei` is also useful as a boundary row. It appears only in the report’s analysis table, without a worked example, and it looks more like a report-only reduplicative-looking extension into adverbial or ability-related territory than a safe first print-facing claim.

# Existing packet boundaries

This packet must keep its boundaries explicit against already completed packets.

## Derivation / valency boundary

`output/publication_review/review_notes_derivation_valency.md` remains the main boundary control for derivational and valency-changing morphology. The reduplication packet should not be widened into a full derivation chapter just because both topics sit in the same broad architecture band.

## Nominalization boundary

`output/publication_review/review_notes_nominalization.md` remains the boundary control for nominalized material. If reduplicative-looking rows start behaving more like lexical nouns or noun-headed forms, they should stay out of the first reduplication slice.

## VP structure and TAM boundary

`output/publication_review/review_notes_vp_structure_stacking.md` and `output/publication_review/review_notes_tam.md` remain the main boundary controls for temporal, iterative, aspectual, and multi-slot verbal interpretation. That is the main reason `leuleu`, `ni ni`, `gengen`, and `theithei` stay boundary-heavy.

## Noun-domain boundary

`output/publication_review/review_notes_noun_domain.md` remains the boundary control for noun-domain material. Rows such as `peuhpeuh` can modify nouns, but that does not make the noun-domain packet the right home for reduplication itself.

# Deferred material

Several kinds of material should remain deferred at this candidate/scoping stage.

1. Aspectual or TAM-heavy rows such as `leuleu` should remain outside the first print-facing claim.
2. VP-facing or temporal syntactic repetition such as `ni ni` should remain outside the first print-facing claim.
3. Thinly evidenced verbal rows such as `gengen` should remain outside the first print-facing claim until they have stronger worked examples.
4. Lexicalized or expressive-looking rows such as `kawikawi` should remain outside the first print-facing claim.
5. Report-only analysis-table rows such as `theithei`, `bangbang`, `bekbek`, `zenzen`, or `tuamtuam` should remain outside the first print-facing claim until they are better controlled.
6. Analyzer-noisy, count-only, or theory-heavy claims about the whole reduplication system should remain outside the first print-facing claim.
7. Any broad derivation chapter claim should remain outside the first slice.
