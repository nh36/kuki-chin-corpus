# What works

The relators / postpositions packet is now aligned at the current slice maturity level. It has a candidate TSV, a scoping dossier, a grammar print slice, a dictionary print slice, and tests covering the current first-slice claims: `candidates_relators_postpositions.tsv`, `dossier_relators_postpositions_scope.md`, `grammar_relators_postpositions_print_slice.md`, `dictionary_relators_postpositions_print_slice.md`, and the associated test files. The packet is controlled by those file-backed editorial layers, not by raw generated-report counts and not by a broad harvest of every relator-like or postposition-like string in `docs/grammar/reports/03-noun-04-relators.md` or `docs/grammar/reports/03-noun-05-postpositions.md`.

This is a narrow relator-led and case-boundary-controlled packet, not a rewrite of the case-marking packet and not a broad postposition inventory. The case-marking packet remains the explicit boundary control: `candidates_case_marking.tsv`, `dossier_case_marking.md`, `grammar_case_marking_print_slice.md`, `dictionary_case_markers_print_slice.md`, and `review_notes_case_marking.md`.

The core first-slice analysis now works in a stable way. `Kiang` is the clearest relator noun for 'beside / near / at the side of'. `Lak` is a usable relator noun for 'among / amid / between'. `Sung` is a usable relator noun for 'inside / within / among'. `Tung` is usable as a relator noun for 'on / above / upon', but only with its case-boundary caveat kept explicit. `Pualam` is usable as a cautious exterior-space relator noun, but only with its lexical-nominal caveat kept explicit. `Pan` is usable as a source postposition only when it is separate or relator-hosted. `Panin` is usable as a source postpositional form, but only with structural and case-boundary caution. `Tawh` is usable as an accompaniment or associative postposition, but only with its case-marking boundary caveat kept explicit.

# What does not yet work

This packet does not rewrite the case-marking treatment of `-ah`, `-in`, `-pan`, `-panin`, or `-tawh`. It does not promote attached or fused-looking forms such as `kiangah`, `sungah`, `tungah`, or `lakpan` as independent postposition entries. It does not promote `nuai` or `mai` as first-slice relator entries. It does not promote `tawhin`, because the evidence is sparse and instrument-like. It does not use `kipan` or `kipanin` rows involving `ki` as clean postposition anchors. It does not use raw generated-report counts as evidence.

# Analyzer/export and overlap caveats

The current caveats are explicit and manageable. `Tung` remains usable, but it stays close to case-marking territory and must keep its case-boundary caveat visible. `Pualam` remains usable, but it stays closer to lexical-nominal behavior than the core relator anchors do. `Pan` remains usable only when it is clearly separate or clearly relator-hosted, not when attached-looking `-pan` strings are being flattened into one entry. `Panin` remains usable only with structural and case-boundary caution, because the packet does not reopen a full compositional analysis beyond the existing case-marking layer.

`Tawh` remains usable only with the case-marking boundary caveat visible across accompaniment and associative uses. `Tawhin` remains deferred because the current evidence is sparse and instrument-like rather than a clean first-slice postposition anchor. `Nuai` remains boundary material because the current evidence stays too close to bare nominal or spatial-noun behavior. `Mai` remains boundary material because it stays too close to lexical noun or body-part material. `Kipan` and `kipanin` remain analyzer/export and report-boundary warnings rather than clean postposition anchors, because rows involving `ki` show why raw export material cannot be promoted directly into print claims.
