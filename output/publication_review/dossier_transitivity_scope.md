# Candidate groups

## Clean intransitives

The safest current intransitive anchors are `sih` and `suak`. They give the packet a small ABS-led intransitive core without immediately forcing locative, agreement, or valency-heavy analysis.

Rows such as `om` and `pai` remain useful background evidence, but they are more case-frame-heavy because locative structure stays visible. `hong` and `ki` are also visible in the report, but they are not currently clean first-slice anchors because their ERG behavior is already mixed enough to raise larger valency questions.

## Clean transitives

The safest current transitive anchors are `hawl` and `en`. They are not perfect, but they are simpler ERG+ABS candidates than more boundary-heavy rows such as `piangsak` or stem-family rows such as `ngai`.

This means the future packet should first test whether a narrow intransitive/transitive contrast can be written with `sih` or `suak` against `hawl`, while keeping larger verb-class theory outside the first slice.

## Alternation and labile material

The report also contains alternation or labile-looking material, especially `mu` / `muh`. Those rows matter because they show that transitivity in the report is not limited to simple binary classes.

They are not safe first-slice anchors, however, because they overlap strongly with Form I / Form II stem alternation. `za` / `zak`, `nei` / `neih`, and similar families sit in the same boundary zone even when they remain useful discovery material.

## Derivation/valency overlap rows

`piangsak` is important because it looks strongly transitive in the report, but it also overlaps directly with causative or valency-changing discussion already controlled by the derivation/valency packet. Report-only rows such as `pia(k)sak` are even less suitable for a first slice.

For that reason, derivation-heavy or causative-looking rows should remain visible in the dossier but outside the first grammar-facing transitivity claim.

## Stem alternation and prefix/case boundary rows

`mu` / `muh`, `za` / `zak`, `nei` / `neih`, and `ngai` / `ngaih` show that the transitivity report frequently touches stem alternation territory.

`nei` / `neih` also overlaps with prefix/agreement territory, because the existing packet already treats `kanei` and related forms as controlled material. `pia`, `gen`, and `tom` show that some report rows are really about argument marking, ditransitives, or case-framed complements rather than the first clean transitivity contrast.

# Existing packet boundaries

The transitivity packet must stay narrow against `output/publication_review/review_notes_derivation_valency.md`, `output/publication_review/review_notes_stem_alternation.md`, `output/publication_review/review_notes_prefix_agreement.md`, `output/publication_review/review_notes_vp_structure_stacking.md`, `output/publication_review/review_notes_tam.md`, and `output/publication_review/review_notes_case_marking.md`.

Those packets already control the main neighboring problems:

- derivation/valency controls `-sak`, wider `-pih` work, `ki-`, and broader valency theory;
- stem alternation controls Form I / Form II family problems;
- prefix/agreement controls object-prefix and agreement-routing material such as `kanei`;
- VP structure and TAM control multi-suffix or aspect-heavy verbal rows;
- case marking controls the interpretation of dative, comitative, and other case-framed arguments.

# Deferred material

The first transitivity slice should defer rows that are lexicalized, analyzer-noisy, report-only, or theoretically unsettled.

`piangsak` remains deferred because it is too derivation-heavy or lexicalized-looking for the first slice.

`ngai` remains deferred because it belongs too closely to a stem-family problem already visible in the stem alternation packet.

`nei` / `neih` remains deferred as a leading claim because it overlaps too directly with prefix/agreement and stem alternation.

`pia`, `gen`, and `tom` remain deferred as leading anchors because they are better read as case-marking or broader argument-structure rows than as the first clean transitivity contrast.

`hong` and `ki` remain deferred as first-slice intransitives because the report evidence already looks too mixed for a narrow clean anchor claim.

`dawt`, `bei`, and report-only `pia(k)sak` rows remain deferred because they are too thin, too noisy, or too dependent on broader valency theory.

analyzer-noisy, lexicalized, or whole-system verb-class claims remain deferred because this packet is not yet ready to become a full verb-class chapter.

The safest next print-facing sub-scope is a narrow grammar print slice for a clean intransitive/transitive contrast, not an ambitransitive/labile system claim and not a stem-alternation boundary claim.
