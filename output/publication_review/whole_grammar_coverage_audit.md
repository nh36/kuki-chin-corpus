# Audit scope

This is an architecture-level audit and planning note, not a new grammar packet and not a printable grammar bundle. It is controlled by `docs/grammar/grammar_source_map.json`, `docs/grammar/GRAMMAR_SOURCE_INVENTORY.md`, `docs/SKELETON_GRAMMAR.md`, `output/grammar/grammar_full.md`, `output/publication_review/remaining_retrofit_inventory.md`, and `output/publication_review/human_review_handoff.md`.

The current publication-review packet sequence is complete for the packets already represented in `output/publication_review/`, but that does **not** mean the whole Tedim grammar is complete at the same maturity level.

# 1. Grammar architecture

The project’s Tedim grammar architecture has several layers, and they do different jobs:

1. `docs/SKELETON_GRAMMAR.md` is the chapter scaffold. It is the broadest whole-grammar outline, spanning phonology, nominal morphology, pronominal systems, verbal morphology, sentence-final material, nominalization, and compounds.
2. `docs/grammar/grammar_source_map.json` is the canonical Tedim topic/construction layer for integrated grammar routing. It is the authoritative machine-readable map for topic-level integration, even though it does **not** by itself prove that every chapter-scale domain has already been lifted into publication-review packets.
3. `docs/grammar/GRAMMAR_SOURCE_INVENTORY.md` is the human-readable map of reports, literature reviews, morpheme files, generated outputs, and analyzer-gap documents. It is the best repository-level source for seeing which grammar domains have evidence but no packet yet.
4. `output/publication_review/` is the candidate-first review layer. A domain reaches the current slice maturity level when it has the aligned packet surfaces appropriate to its packet type: candidate TSV, dossier, grammar print slice, review notes, and tests, plus a dictionary print slice when the packet is lexical or entry-based. Constructional packets such as VP structure / suffix stacking may stop short of an ordinary dictionary slice when the safe claim is about ordering or construction rather than about a headword entry.
5. `output/grammar/grammar_full.md` is a drafting/integration output, not proof of completeness. It is generated from the source map and backend integration layer, and it remains useful for spotting routing gaps or thin sections rather than for claiming that the whole Tedim grammar is editorially complete.

`output/publication_review/remaining_retrofit_inventory.md` is therefore useful but too narrow to stand in for the whole architecture by itself. It tracks the recent publication-review retrofit sequence, not the entire grammar scaffold represented by the skeleton grammar, source inventory, and generated full grammar.

# 4. Audit conclusion

The whole Tedim grammar is **not** yet complete at the same maturity level as the finished publication-review packets. The packet set is strong and internally coherent for the domains already lifted, but large architecture domains still remain only at report, literature, morpheme, or generated-draft level.

`output/grammar/grammar_full.md` therefore cannot be used as proof that the grammar is complete. It is a generated drafting/integration layer whose own headings make clear that many broader chapters still depend on thin routing, partial topic mappings, or human review notes rather than completed candidate-first packets.

# 5. Recommended next step

Do **not** print a full Tedim grammar bundle yet.

The next concrete workflow should be a coverage/priority decision before printing a full grammar bundle. In practice, that means using this audit as a chapter-level coverage matrix and selecting which missing domains should receive the same candidate-first treatment next.

That first-band follow-on selection has now advanced to `candidates_vp_structure_stacking.tsv`, `dossier_vp_structure_stacking_scope.md`, `grammar_vp_structure_stacking_print_slice.md`, and the constructional `review_notes_vp_structure_stacking.md`. This still does **not** make the full grammar print-ready, and it still does **not** recommend assembling the printable grammar bundle yet.

Recommended priority bands:

1. First band: VP structure, suffix combinations/stacking, derivation and valency, pronominal prefixes/agreement/object-prefix systems, and subordination/switch-reference/relative-clause domains.
2. Second band: phonology/tone, nominalization, NP structure/possession, and noun-domain topics such as simple nouns and compounds.
3. Third band: proper nouns and any broader discourse chapter beyond the current sentence-final slice.

Only after that coverage/priority decision should new packet candidates be opened. This audit does **not** recommend assembling the printable grammar bundle yet.
