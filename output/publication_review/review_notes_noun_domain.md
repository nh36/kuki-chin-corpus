# What works

The noun domain section is now normalized into publication-facing prose rather than a narrow packet. It can safely discuss:

- simple lexical noun stems such as `gam` and `aksi`;
- plural marking with `-te` for controlled anchors such as `aksi-te` and `mite`;
- human noun `mi` as a lexical noun head reused in `mi khat`, `mi khempeuh`, `mi pawlkhat`, and `mi tampi`;
- nouns as heads inside counted and quantified phrases;
- a cautious compound boundary note using `minam`.

The section now has a compact noun-domain inventory and multiple formal examples, so it is usable in the assembled review preview alongside the normalized numerals, quantifiers, and NP structure / possession sections.

# Gospel search and source balance

Gospel searching produced several useful noun-domain rows. The strongest current Gospel anchors are `ama aksi`, `mi khempeuh`, `ni li`, and the proper-name comparandum `Abraham' suan David`. These keep the section from becoming another Genesis-only slice.

The cleanest `gam` row and the cleanest `-te` plural row remain OT-led in the current pass, and `hih mite` is still the strongest controlled human-plural anchor. That source balance is acceptable for now because the Gospel alternatives for those specific constructions were weaker or more entangled with other domains.

# What still needs caution

The normalized noun-domain section still needs explicit caveats in several places.

- Proper names remain only partly normalized; the current section does not claim a full proper-noun system.
- Transparent versus lexicalized compounds still need fuller control.
- Nominalized nouns in `-na` remain shared with the nominalization section.
- Classifier-like nouns, kinship nouns, and possessive noun chains still overlap with numerals, quantifiers, and NP structure / possession.
- Broader plurality patterns are not settled by `aksi-te` and `mite` alone.
- Raw noun lists and analyzer-only noun labels are still not print-ready without checked candidate control.
