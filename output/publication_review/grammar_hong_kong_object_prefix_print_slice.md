# Overview of hong and kong object-prefix or inverse-like marking

`hong` 'come / venitive' remains mixed: some rows look inverse-like, while others are clearly deictic, motion, or formulaic. `kong` '1→2 / direct-like' is the cleaner side of the contrast. The section keeps the clearest rows separate from deictic motion, speech-formula rows, and lexicalized or unclear material.

The safest grammar-facing conclusion is narrow: Tedim has a small participant-oriented prefix domain, but this is not a full agreement chapter and not a full inverse-system chapter. The core comparison comes from a paired Gospel row, supported by a second OT `kong` anchor and by cautious `hongmu` 'see me' / `kongmu` 'I see you' support rows.

# Safest hong and kong rows

(@ex:hk-core-matt4) Matthew 4:9
a. Tedim: Ka mai-ah kunin kei hong bia lecin hihte khempeuh nangma tungah kong pia ding hi, a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: ka | mai-ah | kunin | kei | hong | bia | le-cin | hih-te | khempeuh | nangma | tung-ah | kong | pia | ding | hi | a | ci | hi
c. Gloss: 1SG.PRO | front-LOC | when | 1SG.PRO | 2→1 | worship | COND | these | all | 2SG.PRO | on | 1→2 | give | IRR | DECL | and | say | DECL

The cleanest paired Gospel comparison is `hongbia` 'worship me' versus `kongpia` 'give to you' in Matthew 4:9. That same verse makes the contrast visible in one clause: `hongbia` is the clearest hong-side diagnostic, and `kongpia` is the clearest kong-side diagnostic.

Genesis 41:41 adds `kongkoih` 'place you' as a second OT anchor.

(@ex:hk-core-gen41) Genesis 41:41
a. Tedim: Egypt gam khempeuh a uk dingin nang kong koih khinzo hi.
b. Segmentation: Egypt | gam | khempeuh | a | uk | ding-in | nang | kong | koih | khinzo | hi

Together, these rows are enough to support a narrow participant-oriented prefix pocket without claiming a full system.

# Support but not diagnostic rows

The familiar `hongmu` 'see me' and `kongmu` 'I see you' rows remain useful but not decisive. They show that the participant-oriented contrast is real, but they do not by themselves settle whether every hong token is object-prefixal rather than venitive.

# Boundary with deictic / venitive hong

Motion-heavy rows stay boundary material and should not be promoted as object-prefix diagnostics.

# Boundary with lexicalized or unclear rows

Formulaic, nominalized, and speech-formula rows stay boundary-controlled rather than being promoted as object-prefix diagnostics.

# Boundary with transitivity, valency, and pronoun/agreement overlap

Rows that mainly expose clause type, verb class, or person-marking overlap stay boundary-controlled. This slice does not widen into a full agreement chapter or a full inverse chapter.

# Deferred material

The literature-backed inverse claims remain visible in the background sources, but this slice keeps the domain narrow and leaves the broader system for later review.

# Summary

The safest grammar-facing conclusion is that Tedim has a narrow participant-oriented prefix pocket. `hong` remains mixed between venitive/deictic and inverse-like uses, while `kong` is the cleaner direct-like / 1→2 side. Keep the clearest paired rows central, treat `hongmu` and `kongmu` as supportive, and leave deictic, lexicalized, and speech-formula rows boundary-only.

Raw report counts do not decide the analysis.
