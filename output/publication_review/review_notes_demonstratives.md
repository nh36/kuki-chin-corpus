# What works

This packet is strong enough to serve as a real demonstratives/deixis model for the Tedim publication-review series. The grammar slice keeps the treatment narrow and lets the Bible corpus lead: `hih` is the core proximal form, while `tua` is treated as the core distal/anaphoric form rather than as a merely spatial "that".

The plural forms also now look stable enough for print. `Hihte` and `tuate` behave transparently as DEM + `-te`, and the packet represents them directly rather than burying them under the singular entries. That gives the reader a simple system rather than a scattered set of one-off forms.

The strongest part of the packet is the treatment of constructional extensions. `Hih bangin`, `tua bangin`, `tua ciangin`, and `tua ahih ciangin` are all represented with manually checked Bible examples and with clear discourse functions. That gives the slice real explanatory value without forcing it into a broad TAM or discourse chapter.

# What does not yet work

The generated demonstratives report remains too loose to serve as final print prose. It is still useful as discovery scaffolding, but it overstates the safety of `hi`, overstates exact `hih ciangin`, and promotes at least two bad examples without sufficient corpus checking.

The biggest unresolved issue is `hi`. The dossier and the new packet are right to defer it, because exact-token `hi` is too entangled with copular and sentence-final material to support a clean demonstrative headword yet. This means the demonstratives packet is stable, but not exhaustive.

The other unresolved issue is the relation between Bible `tua` and the literature's `huā`. The relationship is plausible and probably historical, but the present packet does not solve it. It keeps the caution explicit rather than pretending that the orthographic and distributional problem has disappeared.

Most importantly, the packet remains deliberately narrow. It does not attempt a full spatial-deixis inventory, a full copula chapter, or a broad discourse account. That limit is a strength here.

# Citation and source audit

The grammar slice cites bibliography keys that already exist in `literature/bibliography.bib`: `@henderson1965` and `@zamngaihcing2017`. No new bibliographic metadata was needed.

Source use is appropriately layered. The literature review supplies the expectation of a two-way demonstrative system and raises the `tua` / `huā` issue; the generated report shows where the current backend remains too simple; the dossier records the corrected evidence picture; and the aligned Bible corpus supplies the actual print examples.

# Selection-method audit

The analyzer already contains a demonstrative inventory. In `scripts/analyze_morphemes.py`, `DEMONSTRATIVES` includes `hih`, `tua`, `hihte`, and `tuate`, while `AMBIGUOUS_MORPHEMES` separately records that `hi` is ambiguous between declarative and copular uses and that `hih` is ambiguous between the proximal demonstrative and Form II of `hi`. The analyzer and disambiguation layer are therefore real assets for demonstrative work, but they are not the same thing as a construction-level example selector.

The current demonstratives report is not part of the present `make grammar-reports` backend path. There is no dedicated demonstratives generator in the current `Makefile` or `scripts/README` report list, so `docs/grammar/reports/06-func-02-demonstratives.md` behaves as a legacy/static report rather than a report regenerated by the current backend pipeline. Older report-generation helpers such as `report_utils.find_diverse_examples()` still scan raw Bible tokens first and only then call `analyze_word`, which means they can surface candidate strings before any construction-level filtering has happened.

That distinction matters directly for demonstratives. Raw string hits can still pull in false friends such as verbal `hih` in `na hih ciangin`, or noisy `hi` material from copular and sentence-final contexts. The present packet therefore treats raw string hits as candidates only. Every promoted example in the grammar and dictionary slices has been manually checked in verse context, and the deferred items `hi` and exact `hih ciangin` stay deferred because the report-selection layer is not strong enough to promote them automatically.

# Report-correction notes

The generated demonstratives report is useful but too simple. The new packet therefore corrects four report-level problems explicitly.

First, `hi` is no longer treated as a safely settled demonstrative headword. Second, exact `hih ciangin` is no longer presented as a strongly supported temporal demonstrative construction. Third, the old Genesis 6:22 sample is no longer treated as a plain `hih bangin` example, because the aligned verse has `tua bangmahin`. Fourth, John 1:19 is no longer used as a clean `hi` demonstrative/copular example.

This pass keeps the generated report minimal and discovery-oriented rather than trying to turn it into a finished chapter. A short correction note now points readers to the dossier and the grammar slice, and the most misleading lines have been softened so they no longer pull the demonstratives packet in the wrong direction.

# Dictionary integration notes

Dictionary integration now looks strong enough for print. The first-round headword set is clear: `hih`, `tua`, `hihte`, `tuate`, `hih bangin`, `tua bangin`, `tua ciangin`, and `tua ahih ciangin`.

The packet also makes the exclusions clear. `Hi` is deferred rather than forced into a bad headword, and `hih ciangin` is withheld because the current corpus evidence is too noisy. The present dictionary slice is better for being smaller and safer.

The one necessary caveat is again the `tua` / `huā` relation. The dictionary should represent Bible `tua` directly, while keeping the literature comparison explicit in grammar or notes rather than silently normalizing the forms together.

# Decision for next slice

The demonstratives/deixis dossier has now been reviewed and converted into a grammar slice, dictionary slice, and review-note packet. After this cleanup and validation pass, the packet is synchronized enough for the current publication-review workflow.

The next substantive step should be to choose another narrow Tedim print-facing topic rather than reopening demonstratives immediately. Broad TAM, directionals, chrestomathy, Mizo/lus, and the other Kuki-Chin languages should remain deferred, and the unresolved `ei/eite` question should still stay flagged for later person-marking work.
