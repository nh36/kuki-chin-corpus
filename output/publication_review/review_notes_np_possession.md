# What works

- The section is now a normalized publication-facing NP structure / possession section rather than a narrow packet note.
- Structural NP rows remain the safest anchors: `hih mite`, `mi khat`, `ni li`, `mi khempeuh`, `mi pawlkhat`, and `mi tampi` support a controlled contrast between demonstrative-before-noun and noun-plus-postnominal numeral or quantifier structure.
- The numerals and quantifiers sections now supply controlled supporting examples, so the NP section can cite noun-plus-numeral and noun-plus-quantifier order without reopening those chapters.
- Possession is now printable with caveats through checked rows such as `na pa' inn-ah` and `a zi' min`, but the subsection still stops short of a full possession paradigm.

# Gospel search and source balance

- Gospel material is now present in the normalized section through `ni li` (John 11:39), `mi khempeuh` (Luke 2:1), plus supporting noun-plus-quantifier rows such as `mi pawlkhat` (Matthew 2:1) and `mi tampi` (Mark 6:34).
- `hih mite` remains the clearest demonstrative-related NP anchor and is still OT-led; Matthew 6:2 gives a less clean Gospel comparandum in `a hih mite`, but it is embedded in a larger phrase.
- No equally clean Gospel possession example was found for the current pass under candidate discipline, so the possession subsection remains OT-led even though the section as a whole is no longer Genesis-heavy.

# What still needs caution

- The section still depends on candidate evidence and explicit caveats rather than on report-level counts.
- The possession side remains thinner and more boundary-heavy than the modifier-order rows.
- No clean full possessive paradigm is yet printed.
- Boundaries with pronouns, possessive prefixes, genitive or apostrophe analysis, relators or postpositions, and broader possession theory remain explicit.
- `Topa' inn`, `a pa' inn`, `Topa' tungah`, and `ka suahna leitang` remain useful controls, but they should not be promoted as if the whole possession system were already normalized.
