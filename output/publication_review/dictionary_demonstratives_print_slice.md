# Scope

These entries give a compact dictionary treatment of the demonstratives slice. Each entry gives the construction or form, a gloss, a status, and one or two manually checked Bible examples. The goal is not to list every deictic-looking string in the corpus, but to represent the stable demonstrative core and its strongest constructional extensions. `Hi`, exact `hih ciangin`, and the relation between `tua` and literature `hua` are deferred rather than treated as settled headwords here.

## hih

Category: demonstrative

Gloss: PROX

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Core demonstratives: `hih` and `tua`"*

Status: stable as the core proximal demonstrative

`Hih` is the safest starting point for the proximal side of the demonstrative system. It works both in identificational expressions such as `hih pen` and as an adnominal determiner before nouns such as `hih mite`. The entry should present it as the ordinary proximal form without trying to absorb shorter `hi`-material into the same headword.

(@dict:dem-hih-1) Genesis 5:1
a. Tedim: Hih pen Adam’ suanlekhakte’ laibu ahi hi.
b. Segmentation: hih pen
c. Gloss: PROX TOP
d. Translation: "This is the book of the generations of Adam."

(@dict:dem-hih-2) Exodus 32:9
a. Tedim: Topa in Moses kiangah, “Hih mite ka mu zo hi. En in, a lungkhauh mite ahi uh hi.
b. Segmentation: hih mite
c. Gloss: PROX people
d. Translation: "And the LORD said unto Moses, I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people:"

## tua

Category: demonstrative

Gloss: DIST; ANAPH

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Core demonstratives: `hih` and `tua`"*

Status: stable with caveat about the `tua` / `hua` relation

`Tua` is the core distal/anaphoric demonstrative in the Bible corpus. It works in straightforward adnominal uses, but it also extends naturally into narrative and discourse-anaphoric reference. The caveat is editorial rather than evidential: Zam Ngaih Cing's distal `huā` is plausibly related, but the Bible corpus overwhelmingly uses `tua`, so the relation should remain explicit rather than silently flattened.

(@dict:dem-tua-1) Genesis 1:6
a. Tedim: Pasian in, “Tuite’ laizangah van kuumpi om hen la, tua van kuumpi in tui le tui kikhensak hen,” ci hi.
b. Segmentation: tua van kuumpi
c. Gloss: DIST firmament
d. Translation: "And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters."

(@dict:dem-tua-2) Genesis 21:27
a. Tedim: Tua ahih ciangin Abraham in tuu le bawngtalte la-in Abimelek pia a, tua mi nihte in thuciamna khat a bawl uh hi.
b. Segmentation: tua mi nihte
c. Gloss: DIST person two-PL
d. Translation: "And Abraham took sheep and oxen, and gave them unto Abimelech; and both of them made a covenant."

## hihte

Category: plural demonstrative

Gloss: PROX-PL

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Plural forms: `hihte` and `tuate`"*

Status: stable as a transparent proximal plural

`Hihte` is a transparent plural extension of `hih`. It works both as a topic-like nominal form and as an independent pronoun. The entry should treat it directly, but without pretending that it requires a separate morphology beyond DEM + `-te`.

(@dict:dem-hihte-1) Genesis 10:20
a. Tedim: Hihte pen a innkuan, a kam pau, a leitang, a minamin Ham’ suanlekhakte ahi hi.
b. Segmentation: hihte pen
c. Gloss: PROX-PL TOP
d. Translation: "These are the sons of Ham, after their families, after their tongues, in their countries, and in their nations."

(@dict:dem-hihte-2) Genesis 48:8
a. Tedim: Israel in Josef’ tapate a muh ciangin amah in, “Hihte kua ahi hiam?” a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: hihte
c. Gloss: PROX-PL
d. Translation: "And Israel beheld Joseph's sons, and said, Who are these?"

## tuate

Category: plural demonstrative

Gloss: DIST-PL

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Plural forms: `hihte` and `tuate`"*

Status: stable as a transparent distal plural

`Tuate` is the distal plural counterpart to `hihte`. It is especially useful in pronominal and discourse-linked reference, including phrases with postpositions. Like `hihte`, it is best treated as a transparent plural demonstrative rather than as a separate lexical subsystem.

(@dict:dem-tuate-1) Genesis 2:19
a. Tedim: Tua ahih ciangin Topa Pasian in gam sung ganhing khempeuh le huih lak vasa khempeuh lei tawh bawl a, mipa in tuate bang a ci hiam ci-in a theihna dingin mipa kiangah tuate a paipih hi.
b. Segmentation: tuate
c. Gloss: DIST-PL
d. Translation: "And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof."

(@dict:dem-tuate-2) Genesis 7:20
a. Tedim: Mual liante tungah tui omto lai a, tuate tungah pi sawmnih le nih thuk-in tuumcip hi.
b. Segmentation: tuate tungah
c. Gloss: DIST-PL on-LOC
d. Translation: "Fifteen cubits upward did the waters prevail; and the mountains were covered."

## hih bangin

Category: demonstrative manner/discourse construction

Gloss: PROX like-CVB

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Manner constructions with `bangin`"*

Status: usable as a stable manner/discourse construction

`Hih bangin` is the strongest proximal manner extension in the present corpus. It is especially common in instructions, quoted speech, and procedural discourse, where it means "like this" or "thus". The entry should be constructional, not a second basic demonstrative headword.

(@dict:dem-hih-bangin-1) Genesis 32:4
a. Tedim: amaute hilhin, “Note in ka topa Esau kiangah hih bangin na ci ding uh hi: Na nasempa Jakob in, ‘Laban’ kiangah peemta-in tu ciang dong ka om hi.
b. Segmentation: hih bangin
c. Gloss: PROX like-CVB
d. Translation: "And he commanded them, saying, Thus shall ye speak unto my lord Esau; Thy servant Jacob saith thus, I have sojourned with Laban, and stayed there until now:"

(@dict:dem-hih-bangin-2) Genesis 42:18
a. Tedim: A ni thum ni-in Josef in amaute kiangah, “Keimah in Pasian zahtak ka hih manin hih bangin gamta un. Tua hileh na nungta ding uh hi.
b. Segmentation: hih bangin
c. Gloss: PROX like-CVB
d. Translation: "And Joseph said unto them the third day, This do, and live; for I fear God:"

## tua bangin

Category: demonstrative manner/discourse construction

Gloss: DIST like-CVB

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Manner constructions with `bangin`"*

Status: usable as a stable manner/discourse construction

`Tua bangin` is the distal/anaphoric counterpart to `hih bangin`. It often resumes, summarizes, or evaluates an already described event. That gives it a strong discourse function in addition to its basic manner sense.

(@dict:dem-tua-bangin-1) Exodus 14:30
a. Tedim: Topa in tua bangin Egypt mite’ khutsung panin tua ni-in Israel-te honkhia a, tuipi gei-ah Egypt mi a site, Israel mite in mu uh hi.
b. Segmentation: tua bangin
c. Gloss: DIST like-CVB
d. Translation: "Thus the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians; and Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore."

(@dict:dem-tua-bangin-2) Genesis 50:21
a. Tedim: Tua ahih ciangin lau kei un. Note le na tate uh a dingin an kong pia ding hi,” a ci hi. Amah in tua bangin kip gen pha kikin amaute a hehnem hi.
b. Segmentation: tua bangin
c. Gloss: DIST like-CVB
d. Translation: "Now therefore fear ye not: I will nourish you, and your little ones. And he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them."

## tua ciangin

Category: temporal/discourse demonstrative construction

Gloss: DIST time-CVB

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Discourse and temporal deixis"*

Status: stable as a temporal/discourse linker

`Tua ciangin` is one of the strongest demonstrative constructions in the Bible corpus. It regularly marks temporal progression and event sequencing, with English translations such as "then" or "at that time". This is the construction the current packet uses to represent demonstrative temporal linkage; exact `hih ciangin` is deferred.

(@dict:dem-tua-ciangin-1) Genesis 1:3
a. Tedim: Pasian in, “Khuavak om hen,” ci hi; tua ciangin khuavak om pah hi.
b. Segmentation: tua ciangin
c. Gloss: DIST time-CVB
d. Translation: "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light."

(@dict:dem-tua-ciangin-2) Genesis 5:5
a. Tedim: Tua ahih ciangin Adam’ nuntak hun sungteng pen kum zakua le kum sawmthum hi a, tua ciangin amah si hi.
b. Segmentation: tua ciangin
c. Gloss: DIST time-CVB
d. Translation: "and all the days that Adam lived were nine hundred and thirty years: and he died."

## tua ahih ciangin

Category: discourse-temporal demonstrative construction

Gloss: DIST be.3SG then-CVB

Cross-reference: *Grammar slice, "Discourse and temporal deixis"*

Status: usable as a stable discourse-transition construction

`Tua ahih ciangin` is a fixed discourse bridge that links a prior state or event to what follows. It is more specific than plain `tua ciangin`, and it is one of the clearest ways the distal/anaphoric demonstrative extends into narrative and expository structure.

(@dict:dem-tua-ahih-ciangin-1) Genesis 2:19
a. Tedim: Tua ahih ciangin Topa Pasian in gam sung ganhing khempeuh le huih lak vasa khempeuh lei tawh bawl a, mipa in tuate bang a ci hiam ci-in a theihna dingin mipa kiangah tuate a paipih hi.
b. Segmentation: tua ahih ciangin
c. Gloss: DIST be.3SG then-CVB
d. Translation: "And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof."

(@dict:dem-tua-ahih-ciangin-2) Genesis 2:21
a. Tedim: Tua ahih ciangin Topa Pasian in mipa ihmut suak mahmah sak a, ama ihmut kalin a nakguhte khat la-in tua mun pen satak tawh a dimsak hi.
b. Segmentation: tua ahih ciangin
c. Gloss: DIST be.3SG then-CVB
d. Translation: "And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof;"
