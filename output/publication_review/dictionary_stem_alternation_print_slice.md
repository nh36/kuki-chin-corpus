# Editorial scope

These entries are short print-facing dictionary drafts for the stem-alternation slice. Each entry gives the paired stem forms, a reader-facing gloss, a cautious valency note where possible, an editorial status, and one or two manually checked Bible examples. The goal is not to list every form in the backend, but to show how alternating verbs can be represented cleanly in print.

## mu ~ muh

Category: verb; perception; often transitive

Stem forms: Form I `mu`, Form II `muh`

Gloss: "see"

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Main environments for stem alternation"*

Status: draft-ready

`mu ~ muh` is one of the clearest pairs for print. `mu` is straightforward in finite predication, while `muh` is well supported in nominalized dependent expressions such as `muhna-ah` "in [your] sight". The print dictionary should therefore keep the pair together rather than treating `muh` as a separate lexeme.

(@dict:mu-1)
a. Tedim: Pasian in tua khuavak hoih hi, ci-in a mu hi. Pasian in khuamial panin khuavak khenkhia hi.
b. Segmentation: mu
c. Gloss: see.I
d. Translation: "And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness."

(@dict:mu-2)
a. Tedim: en un, note' muhna-ah na nasempa in maipha muzo a, ka nuntakna nong hutna uhah migitna lianpi kei tungah nong lak khin uh hi.
b. Segmentation: muh-na
c. Gloss: see.II-NMLZ
d. Translation: "behold now, thy servant hath found grace in thy sight, and thou hast magnified thy mercy, which thou hast shewed unto me in saving my life"

## ne ~ nek

Category: verb; ingestion; transitive

Stem forms: Form I `ne`, Form II `nek`

Gloss: "eat"

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Main environments for stem alternation"*

Status: draft-ready

`ne ~ nek` is unusually good dictionary evidence because Genesis 2:17 places both forms in one verse. The finite prohibition uses `ne`, while the dependent temporal clause uses `nek`. That makes the pair easy to explain in print without forcing a speculative rule beyond the actual verse context.

(@dict:ne-1)
a. Tedim: Ahih hangin a pha le a sia theihna singkung gah pen na ne kei ding hi. Bang hang hiam cih leh tua na nek ni-in na si ding hi, a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: ne ... nek
c. Gloss: eat.I ... eat.II
d. Translation: "but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die"

## nei ~ neih

Category: verb; possession; often transitive or predicative

Stem forms: Form I `nei`, Form II `neih`

Gloss: "have, possess"

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Stem alternation and clause/sentence structure"*

Status: draft-ready

The pair `nei ~ neih` is also ready for print. `nei` works as the ordinary finite stem 'have', while `neih` is well attested in derived nominal expressions. A paired entry is more informative than two separate lemmata, because the key point is the structural distribution of one lexical verb.

(@dict:nei-1)
a. Tedim: Tu-in Sarai ciing a, ta nei lo hi.
b. Segmentation: nei
c. Gloss: have.I
d. Translation: "But Sarai was barren; she had no child."

(@dict:nei-2)
a. Tedim: David' neih mi thahatte' minte: Tahkhemon mi Joshebbasshebeth hi a, amah pen mi thumte a ukpa ahi hi.
b. Segmentation: nei-h
c. Gloss: have.II-NOM
d. Translation: "These be the names of the mighty men whom David had: the Tachmonite that sat in the seat, chief among the captains"

## thei ~ theih

Category: verb; cognition; usually transitive or complement-taking

Stem forms: Form I `thei`, Form II `theih`

Gloss: "know, understand"

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Main environments for stem alternation"*

Status: analytically provisional

The literature and the current lexicon support `thei ~ theih` as a real pair, but the Bible corpus most often surfaces the Form II side through derived forms such as `theihna`. The finite Form I side also overlaps with ability-style uses such as `ne thei`, and the current questionnaire duplicates the pair with conflicting labels. The print entry should therefore keep the pair together but flag its exact distribution for further review.

(@dict:thei-1)
a. Tedim: Numei in gulpi kiangah, Huan sung singgahte ka ne thei ding uh hi.
b. Segmentation: thei
c. Gloss: know.I
d. Translation: "And the woman said unto the serpent, We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden:"

(@dict:thei-2)
a. Tedim: Ahih hangin a pha le a sia theihna singkung gah pen na ne kei ding hi. Bang hang hiam cih leh tua na nek ni-in na si ding hi, a ci hi.
b. Segmentation: theih-na
c. Gloss: know.II-NMLZ
d. Translation: "but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die"

## piang ~ pian

Category: verb; intransitive and derived nominal uses

Stem forms: Form I `piang`, Form II `pian`

Gloss: "be born; come about"

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Stem alternation and clause/sentence structure"*

Status: needs review

`piang ~ pian` should be represented in the dictionary, but with a more cautious status than `mu ~ muh` or `ne ~ nek`. The Bible corpus shows Form I clearly in clause-final expressions such as `a piang pah hi`, while the Form II side is more often visible through forms such as `pianna` or `a pian nadingin`. The pair is real, but the most reader-friendly way to explain it still needs a little editorial settling.

(@dict:piang-1)
a. Tedim: Pasian in, "Vantungte nuai-a om tuite mun khatah kikhawm hen la, lei keu kidawk hen," ci hi. Tua mah bangin a piang pah hi.
b. Segmentation: piang
c. Gloss: be.born.I / come.to.pass.I
d. Translation: "And God said, Let the waters under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so."

(@dict:piang-2)
a. Tedim: Ofir, Havilah, le Jobab' pianna pa ahi hi. Hihte khempeuh Joktan' tapate ahi hi.
b. Segmentation: pian-na
c. Gloss: be.born.II-NMLZ
d. Translation: "and Ophir, and Havilah, and Jobab: all these were the sons of Joktan."

## pia ~ piak

Category: verb; transfer; ditransitive

Stem forms: Form I `pia`, Form II `piak`

Gloss: "give"

Cross-reference: *Grammar review slice, "Stem alternation and transitivity"*

Status: needs review

Direct corpus evidence supports `pia ~ piak`, and Genesis 3:12 is a particularly useful print example because it contains both forms in one speech turn. The difficulty is not the pair itself, but the noisier autogenerated report layer around it. For that reason the entry is safe enough to print as a manually curated pair, but not yet as a fully settled backend-derived paradigm.

(@dict:pia-1)
a. Tedim: Mipa in, "Kei tawh a om dinga nong piak numei in singgah hong pia a, ke'n ka ne hi," ci hi.
b. Segmentation: piak ... pia
c. Gloss: give.II ... give.I
d. Translation: "And the man said, The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat."
