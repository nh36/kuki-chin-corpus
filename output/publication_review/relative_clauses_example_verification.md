# 1. `a bawl mi` – Promoted diagnostic

**Source reference:** Exodus 30:38

**Full Bible context:**
> Tua tawh a kibangin paknamtui a bawl mi peuhmah ama mite' kiang panin kikhenkhia ding hi," a ci hi.

**Target span:** `a bawl mi`

**Exact Tedim text:** `paknamtui a bawl mi peuhmah`

**Segmentation:**
- `paknamtui` = perfume
- `a` = 3SG.REL (agreement/relativizer)
- `bawl` = make
- `mi` = person
- `peuhmah` = every

**Gloss:** perfume | 3SG.REL | make | person | every

**Translation:** "whoever makes any like it" or "any person who makes perfume like it"

**Head or head-like element:** `mi` (person)

**Relative-clause span:** `a bawl` (the predicate "makes")

**Role of head inside relative clause:** The head `mi` (person) is the subject of the relative clause. The relative predicate `a bawl` (he/she makes) takes the head as its implicit subject.

**Status of `mi`:** In this construction, `mi` functions as a **head noun** (the noun being modified) rather than as a nominalizer or relativizer per se. The prefix `a-` marks ordinary 3SG agreement with the understood (or fronted) subject. The relative reading emerges from the prenominal position of the predicate and the definiteness of `a-`.

**Construction type:** `mi`-headed relative-like construction with a-marked predicate.

**Display assessment:**

The example is clean and representative:
- The relative span `a bawl` is clearly demarcated
- The head noun `mi` is transparent
- The translation "person who makes" accurately reflects the construction
- The longer verse context is needed here because we're showing how this fits into an actual sentence; however, the key example `paknamtui a bawl mi` is compact enough to stand on its own with the source reference

**Recommendation:** **Remain promoted.** This is a good first diagnostic for `mi`-headed relative-like constructions. The analysis is clean: `a-` is ordinary 3SG agreement (not a unique relativizer), and `mi` is the head noun. The construction is prenominal (clause before head) and transparent.

**Caveat to record:** The prefix `a-` is also ordinary 3SG agreement in many other contexts (e.g., simple predicates), so it is not a unique relative-clause marker. However, in the prenominal position with a head noun, the relative reading is robust.

# 2. `omte` – Supporting evidence

**Source reference:** Genesis 2:1

**Full Bible context:**
> Tua ahih ciangin vantung, leitung, le a sunga omte khempeuh kiman khin hi.

**Target span:** `omte`

**Exact Tedim text:** `a sunga omte khempeuh`

**Segmentation:**
- `a` = 3SG
- `sunga` = inside
- `om-te` = exist-PL
- `khempeuh` = all

**Gloss:** 3SG | inside | exist-PL | all

**Translation:** "all that was in it" or "all those that exist/are in it"

**Head or head-like element:** None (headless/free relative reading) OR `khempeuh` (all) functioning as a partitive head

**Relative-clause span:** `om-te` (the predicate "exist-PL")

**Role of head inside relative clause:** If headless/free relative: the form refers to participants within the domain. If nominalization: `omte` is a nominalized state/existence + plural.

**Status of `mi`:** Not applicable. This construction uses `om` (exist) + plural `-te`, not `mi`.

**Construction type:** Ambiguous. The form could be:

1. **Headless/free relative:** "those who/that exist" → supports a relative-clause reading
2. **Plural nominalization:** "existents" or "those-that-are" → supports a nominalization reading
3. **Existential + plural marking:** a straightforward predicate with number agreement

**Verification of construction:** In Genesis 2:1 context, "a sunga omte khempeuh" follows "vantung, leitung" (heavens, earth) and describes what was "inside" these domains. The translation "all that was in it" supports a relative reading. However, the form could also be understood as simply "all the existents/beings" (nominalized plural). The ambiguity cannot be fully resolved from the context alone.

**Display assessment:**

The example is transparently segmented, but the construction type is genuinely ambiguous:
- The form is source-resolved and transparent
- The translation supports a relative interpretation
- However, it could equally well be nominalized plural
- Keeping it as **supporting** (not promoted) is the right call given this ambiguity

**Recommendation:** **Remain supporting.** The form is source-resolved and the context is clear, but the construction type (headless/free relative vs plural nominalization) is unresolved enough to keep it as support rather than diagnostic. This is appropriate because it illustrates the relative-like domain without claiming a full analysis.

**Caveat to record:** The form `omte` (om-te) is ambiguous between a headless/free-relative and a plural nominalization of the existential. Until the report clarifies the relative ratio, this remains support.

# 3. `omna` – Nominalization boundary

**Source reference:** Genesis 4:16

**Full Bible context:**
> Tua ciangin Topa' omna panin Kain paikhia-in Eden nisuahna lam Nod gam sungah a teng hi.

**Target span:** `omna`

**Exact Tedim text:** `Topa' omna panin`

**Segmentation:**
- `Topa'` = LORD (possessive)
- `om-na` = exist-NMLZ
- `panin` = ABL (from/away from)

**Gloss:** LORD.POSS | exist-NMLZ | ABL

**Translation:** "from the presence/sight of the LORD" (lit. "from the Lord's being/existing")

**Head or head-like element:** The nominalized frame `omna` (the existing/presence of the Lord)

**Relative-clause span:** The entire nominalized construction `omna` does not have a distinct relative clause. The construction itself is nominalized.

**Role of head inside relative clause:** Not applicable. This is not a relative clause; it is a nominalized locative/temporal frame.

**Status of `mi`:** Not applicable. This uses `om` (exist) + nominalizer `-na`, not `mi`.

**Construction type:** Nominalized locative construction. `omna` is "place/time of existing" or "presence". The `-na` suffix creates a nominal form, and the whole construction describes a location (figuratively, the "presence" or "sight" of the Lord).

**Display assessment:**

This is clearly **nominalization, not a relative clause**:
- The `-na` is an explicit nominalizer
- The construction creates a noun phrase describing a location/state
- No relative-clause structure is present
- The example belongs entirely in the nominalization section

**Recommendation:** **Remain boundary material.** This should NOT be promoted to a relative-clause diagnostic. It is a clean example of nominalized `om-na`, but it belongs with the nominalization packet, not with the relative-clause core. The packet correctly identifies this as a nominalization boundary.

**Caveat to record:** `omna` is a high-frequency form (474 tokens in the report) and represents a clear nominalization pattern. It should remain boundary material and be referenced in the nominalization section.

# 4. `muhna-ah` – Case-marked nominalization boundary

**Source reference:** Genesis 6:11

**Full Bible context:**
> Tu-in Pasian' muhna-ah leitung sia a, leitungah ngongtatna kidim hi.

**Target span:** `muhna-ah`

**Exact Tedim text:** `Pasian' muhna-ah`

**Segmentation:**
- `Pasian'` = God (genitive/possessive)
- `muh-na-ah` = see-NMLZ-LOC

**Gloss:** God.GEN | see-NMLZ-LOC

**Translation:** "in the sight of God" (lit. "in God's seeing/being-seen")

**Head or head-like element:** The case-marked nominalized form `muhna-ah` (the sight/seeing of God)

**Relative-clause span:** Not a relative clause. The construction is nominalized + case-marked.

**Role of head inside relative clause:** Not applicable.

**Status of `mi`:** Not applicable. This uses `muh` (see) + nominalizer `-na` + locative case `-ah`, not `mi`.

**Construction type:** Case-marked nominalization. The form `muhna` is a nominalized predicate (the state/act of seeing), and `-ah` marks it as locative/case-marked (in the sight of).

**Display assessment:**

This is clearly **case-marked nominalization, not a relative clause**:
- The `-na` nominalizes the predicate `muh`
- The `-ah` case-marks the nominalized form for locative/directional meaning
- The construction is morphosyntactically distinct from relative clauses
- It represents an interaction between nominalization and case marking

**Recommendation:** **Remain boundary material.** This is correctly kept as a case-marked nominalization boundary. The example is source-resolved and clean, but it demonstrates the overlap between nominalization and case marking, not a relative-clause structure. It belongs with the case-marking section and the nominalization section, not with the relative-clause core.

**Caveat to record:** `muhna-ah` (and related `-na-ah` forms) are clear boundaries between the relative-clause domain and the nominalization+case domain. This should be explicitly kept separate.
