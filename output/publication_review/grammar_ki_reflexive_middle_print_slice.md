# Overview of `ki-` reflexive / reciprocal / middle-like marking

This section isolates `ki-` 'reflexive / reciprocal / middle-like' so that voice-like evidence is no longer scattered across unrelated chapters. The first claim is deliberately narrow: reciprocal `ki-gawm` 'join together' and subject-affected `kilaktoh` 'be taken up' are the clearest currently promoted constructions.

The forms `kisep` 'REFL-work / do by oneself' and `kigen` 'be said / be told' remain useful but cautious support because lexicalization risk remains real. This section therefore does not claim a full voice chapter, a full transitivity chapter, a full prefix/agreement chapter, a full derivation chapter, or a full VP-slot template.

# Current `ki-` inventory

| Form or pattern | Rough function | Example context | Current grammar-facing status | Boundary issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ki-gawm` | reciprocal predicate | spouse-pair and dual-participant clauses | supported core | reciprocal evidence does not settle all `ki-` behavior |
| `kilaktoh` | middle or subject-affected predicate | "be taken up" rows | supported with caution | overlaps directionals and VP stacking |
| `kigen` | passive-like or agent-defocused reporting | "be told / cannot be told" rows | supported with caution | analyzer glossing is partly lexicalized |
| `kisep` | reflexive or self-directed support row | "work done" rows | supporting candidate | can be lexicalized in procedural discourse |

| `ki-phuak`, `ki-piak-na` | `ki-` with derivational or VP layering | naming and nominalized-event rows | boundary material | overlaps TAM, nominalization, and valency |
| report-only `kibawl` counts | discovery-only inventory | count-heavy report tables | blocked | count totals are not constructional proof |

# Safest reflexive / reciprocal / middle-like evidence

Reciprocal `ki-gawm` 'join together' is the strongest starting point because participant structure is explicit and parallel across Old Testament and Gospel examples.

(@ex:ki-rec-kigawm-gen2) Genesis 2:24
a. Tedim: a zi tawh kigawm a, amau tegel pum khat a bang uh hi.
b. Segmentation: a | zi | tawh | ki-gawm | a | amau | tegel | pum | khat | a | bang | uh | hi
c. Gloss: 3SG.POSS | wife | COM | RECP-join | CONJ | 3PL.PRO | pair | body | one | GEN | become | PL | DECL
d. Translation: He is joined with his wife, and the two become one body. (Genesis 2:24)

Matthew 19:5 provides the Gospel counterpart with the same reciprocal predicate.

(@ex:ki-rec-kigawm-matt19) Matthew 19:5
a. Tedim: a zi tawh kigawm ding a, amau tegel pumkhat a bang ding uh hi.
b. Segmentation: a | zi | tawh | ki-gawm | ding | a | amau | tegel | pum-khat | a | bang | ding | uh | hi
c. Gloss: 3SG.POSS | wife | COM | RECP-join | IRR | CONJ | 3PL.PRO | pair | body-one | GEN | become | IRR | PL | DECL
d. Translation: He will be joined with his wife, and the two will become one body. (Matthew 19:5)

Subject-affected `kilaktoh` 'be taken up' supplies the cleanest middle-like support now available, while still keeping directional and VP overlap visible.

(@ex:ki-mid-kilaktoh-num9) Numbers 9:17
a. Tedim: kilaktoh
b. Segmentation: ki-lak-toh
c. Gloss: REFL-take-UP
d. Translation: was taken up (Numbers 9:17)

Luke 9:51 gives the Gospel counterpart for the same subject-affected construction.

(@ex:ki-mid-kilaktoh-luke9) Luke 9:51
a. Tedim: a kilaktoh ding hun
b. Segmentation: a | ki-lak-toh | ding | hun
c. Gloss: 3SG | REFL-take-UP | IRR | time
d. Translation: the time for him to be taken up (Luke 9:51)

# Passive-like or agent-defocused evidence

Passive-like or agent-defocused `kigen` 'be said / be told' is useful, but it must remain cautious because lexicalized reporting uses are common.

(@ex:ki-pass-kigen-gen27) Genesis 27:42
a. Tedim: Rebekah tungah kigen hi.
b. Segmentation: Rebekah | tung-ah | ki-gen | hi
c. Gloss: Rebekah | on-LOC | REFL-say | DECL
d. Translation: The words were told to Rebekah. (Genesis 27:42)

Matthew 2:10 keeps the same `kigen` line in a Gospel clause with modal negation.

(@ex:ki-pass-kigen-matt2) Matthew 2:10
a. Tedim: a lungdamna uh kigen thei lo hi.
b. Segmentation: a | lungdamna | uh | ki-gen | thei | lo | hi
c. Gloss: 3SG | joy | 2/3PL | REFL-say | can | NEG | DECL
d. Translation: their joy could not be told. (Matthew 2:10)

# Lexicalized or frozen `ki-` boundary

Forms such as `kipan` 'begin / from' remain boundary material in this section. They are important for coverage, but they do not justify promotion into the core reflexive/reciprocal/middle claim.

(@ex:ki-lex-kipan-gen4) Genesis 4:26
a. Tedim: Tua hun a kipanin mite in Topa bia-in a min lawh ding kipan ta uh hi.
b. Segmentation: tua | hun | a | ki-pan-in | mite | in | Topa | bia-in | a | min | lawh | ding | ki-pan | ta | uh | hi
c. Gloss: that | time | 3SG | REFL-begin-ERG | people | ERG | LORD | worship-CVB | 3SG | name | call | IRR | REFL-begin | PFV | 2/3PL | DECL
d. Translation: From that time people began to call on the name of the Lord. (Genesis 4:26)

Matthew 4:17 gives the Gospel counterpart with the same lexicalized begin/from profile.

(@ex:ki-lex-kipan-matt4) Matthew 4:17
a. Tedim: Tua hun a kipan Jesuh in thuhilh ding kipan a.
b. Segmentation: tua | hun | a | ki-pan | Jesuh | in | thu-hilh | ding | ki-pan | a
c. Gloss: that | time | 3SG | REFL-begin | Jesus | ERG | word-teach | IRR | REFL-begin | DECL
d. Translation: From that time Jesus began to preach. (Matthew 4:17)

# Boundary with derivation, VP stacking, transitivity, and prefix/agreement

The forms `ki-phuak` 'be called' and `ki-piak-na` 'REFL-give-NMLZ' show that `ki-` rapidly crosses into derivation, nominalization, TAM layering, and VP stacking. The same is true for overlap with transitivity and prefix/agreement boundaries. For that reason, these rows remain boundary material rather than new core evidence in this first section.

# Deferred and boundary material

Several issues remain outside the present account.

- `ki-gawm` and `kilaktoh` support a controlled first claim, but they do not settle the full voice system.
- `kisep` and `kigen` remain cautious support rows with lexicalization risk.
- `kipan` remains lexicalized or frozen boundary material.
- `ki-` plus derivational, TAM, and VP-heavy material remains boundary material.
- Raw report counts are not treated as grammar facts without constructional control.

# Summary

The safest grammar-facing conclusion is a narrow one: reciprocal `ki-gawm` and subject-affected `kilaktoh` provide clear first anchors for a dedicated `ki-` section, while passive-like `kigen`, supporting `kisep`, lexicalized `kipan`, and derivation/VP overlaps remain explicit boundaries.
