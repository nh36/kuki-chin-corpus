# What works

The quantifiers packet is now aligned at the normalized publication-facing coverage level established in `coverage_normalization_audit.md` and piloted in `grammar_numerals_print_slice.md`. The packet now includes `candidates_quantifiers.tsv`, `dossier_quantifiers.md`, `grammar_quantifiers_print_slice.md`, `review_notes_quantifiers.md`, and the checked supplement `examples_quantifiers_normalization.tsv`. The normalized section remains controlled by candidate evidence, the dossier, the report in `docs/grammar/reports/06-func-05-quantifiers.md`, and explicit caveats rather than by raw generated-report counts or broad string searches.

The core analysis is synchronized in the right way. `Khempeuh` remains the safest universal or total anchor. `Pawlkhat` remains existential or indefinite-like evidence, but still with partitive or grouping caveats. `Mi khat` remains numeral/indefinite boundary evidence and is not treated as an uncomplicated quantifier anchor. `Kuamah` and `bangmah` remain usable only in negative-licensed contexts. `Tampi tak` remains the inherited quantity/degree anchor, while `mi tampi` now gives the normalized section a cleaner noun-phrase quantity row. `Zaw` and `mahmah` remain comparative or intensifier edge rows. `Peuhpeuh` and `tawm` remain deferred and not print-ready.

# Gospel search and source balance

The normalized section no longer depends almost entirely on early Old Testament material. The checked search produced usable Gospel examples for several common quantifier patterns:

- `mi khempeuh` in Luke 2:1 for total quantification;
- `mi pawlkhat` in Matthew 2:1 for existential or indefinite-like quantification;
- `mi tampi` in Mark 6:34 for noun-phrase quantity;
- `kuamah in bangmah hih thei lo hi` in John 3:27 for negative-licensed quantifier interaction.

Matthew 2:3 `Jerusalem khuami khempeuh` also supports the same postnominal total-quantifier pattern, even though the normalized section did not need to print every checked Gospel row.

The Gospel search did not produce a cleaner replacement for the classic `mi khat` boundary row from Genesis 32:24, so that boundary example remains Old Testament-based. Gospel `peuhpeuh` material also continued to behave more like free-choice or discourse-sensitive indefinite material than like a settled core universal or distributive quantifier pattern, so it remains deferred.

# What still needs caution

The packet still needs the same structural cautions, even though the section is now fuller and more publication-facing.

- `pawlkhat` should still be read against the Genesis 32:8 partitive or alternative-grouping anchor, not as a flat article-like `some`;
- `khat` still overlaps with numerals and indefinites, so the numerals section remains the fuller home for `one`;
- `kuamah` and `bangmah` still require negative licensing, so the quantifiers section should cross-reference, not reopen, the stabilized negation packet;
- bang-family false friends such as `tua bangmah hi-in` still need to remain blocked;
- `tampi tak` and `mi tampi` are useful, but they should not expand the packet into a broad adjective/adverb chapter;
- `zaw` and `mahmah` remain edge rows rather than the start of a full degree/intensifier/comparative chapter;
- `peuhpeuh` and `tawm` remain deferred because the current evidence is still too free-choice-heavy or analyzer-noisy for print promotion.

# Current maturity and next use

Quantifiers is now the second normalized coverage section after numerals. The assembled grammar review preview can now include a fuller publication-facing quantifiers chapter while still remaining a review preview rather than a finished grammar.

The packet is ready for human review at the current normalized coverage level. Later quantifier changes should come from a specific reviewer-identified defect or from a deliberately chosen later coverage-normalization pass on a clearly defined subproblem, not from another open-ended polishing pass.

The broader repository priorities remain unchanged. Phonology/tone and verb paradigms remain major non-homogeneous gaps. Coordinators, sentence-final particles, broad TAM, directionals, chrestomathy, Mizo/lus, and other Kuki-Chin language work remain separate editorial priorities rather than material to absorb into this quantifier pass.
