# Scope and status

This is the first candidate/scoping pass for Tedim relator nouns and postpositions. It asks whether relators / postpositions can support a small independent candidate-first packet while keeping the boundary with the existing case-marking packet explicit.

This dossier is therefore **not** a grammar print slice. Grammar, dictionary, and review-note print slices for relators/postpositions have **not** yet begun. `grammar_relators_postpositions_print_slice.md`, `dictionary_relators_postpositions_print_slice.md`, and `review_notes_relators_postpositions.md` do **not** yet exist.

# Evidence protocol

The discovery sources for this pass are `docs/grammar/reports/03-noun-04-relators.md` and `docs/grammar/reports/03-noun-05-postpositions.md`. Those generated reports are discovery sources only. The controlling layer for this packet is `output/publication_review/candidates_relators_postpositions.tsv`.

# Relator-noun candidates

The clearest positive relator-noun candidates are `kiang`, `lak`, `sung`, and `tung`. These forms behave like relational nouns or stems that host locative and source marking, and they are exactly the forms that the case-marking packet already keeps visible in rows such as `kiangah`, `sungah`, `tungah`, and `lakpan`. That makes them good candidates for a later relator-oriented grammar slice: they are not just lexical nouns, but neither are they well described as bare case suffixes.

`Pualam` is also usable as a relator-noun candidate, but with more caution. It has a clean exterior-space meaning and enough bare-plus-locative behavior to stay in the packet, yet it looks slightly more lexical-nominal than the first four anchors.

`Nuai` and `mai` should remain lower-confidence boundary material for now. `Nuai` clearly belongs to the same spatial domain, but the current report mostly shows bare noun-like uses rather than a clean first-slice pattern parallel to `kiang`, `lak`, or `sung`. `Mai` is even more boundary-heavy because it stays very close to ordinary lexical noun or body-part material meaning 'face/front'. Both should remain visible in the candidate layer without being promoted into the first grammar slice automatically.

# Postposition candidates

`Pan`, `panin`, and `tawh` are the cleanest postposition candidates, but they must stay boundary-controlled. `Pan` is usable as a source postposition when it is clearly separate or clearly hosted by a relator noun, as in `kiang pan` or `lak pan`. `Panin` is also usable as a source form candidate, but only with the same structural caution already preserved in the case-marking packet. The question here is not whether `panin` expresses source; it does. The question is how far an independent relators/postpositions packet should go in reanalyzing it beyond the existing case-marking treatment.

`Tawh` is also clean enough to remain in the packet as a postposition candidate, especially for separate accompaniment or associative use. But the case-marking packet already treats `tawh` carefully across accompaniment and material/instrumental extension, so the relators/postpositions packet should treat it as boundary material rather than as a blank-slate new chapter.

`Tawhin` should remain deferred. The report shows that it exists, but it is too sparse and too strongly instrument-like to be a clean first-slice anchor.

# Case-marking boundary

This packet only works if the boundary with case marking stays explicit. The case-marking packet already owns the main print-facing discussion of `-ah`, `-in`, `-pan`, `-panin`, and `-tawh` as marker-like material. It also already keeps relator-hosted constructions visible through rows such as `kiangah`, `sungah`, `tungah`, `lakpan`, `inn panin`, and `kei tawh`.

The relators/postpositions packet should therefore add value in a different place:

1. by treating `kiang`, `lak`, `sung`, `tung`, `pualam`, `nuai`, and `mai` as potential relator nouns rather than as accidental hosts for case suffixes;
2. by distinguishing clearly separate postpositions such as `pan`, `panin`, `tawh`, and `tawhin` from attached or case-like material;
3. by refusing to reopen the case-marking packet's settled claims about `-ah`, `-in`, and the already reviewed source/comitative markers.

The practical boundary is especially important around `-ah`, `-in`, `pan/panin`, and `tawh`. Attached or fused-looking `kiangah`, `sungah`, `tungah`, `lakpan`, and similar forms are already adequately handled inside the case-marking packet. A new relators/postpositions slice should use them as boundary evidence, not as a reason to rewrite case marking.

# Deferred or boundary material

Several forms should remain only as boundary evidence for now.

- Raw report counts are not evidence and should not drive promotion decisions.
- `Mai` and `nuai` remain lower-confidence relator candidates because the current report leaves them close to lexical noun territory.
- `Tawhin` remains deferred because the current report gives only sparse instrumental-looking evidence.
- Report rows involving `ki` (`kipan`, `kipanin`) show why raw report output cannot simply be read as a postposition list.
- Attached or fused-looking rows such as `kiangah`, `sungah`, `tungah`, and `lakpan` remain shared boundary territory with the case-marking packet rather than independent postposition anchors.

# Safest next print-facing sub-scope

The safest next print-facing sub-scope is a combined but narrow relators/postpositions grammar slice, led by relator nouns rather than by a broad postposition inventory. That slice should center on `kiang`, `lak`, `sung`, `tung`, and cautiously `pualam`, while treating `pan`, `panin`, and `tawh` only where they are clearly separate or clearly postpositional rather than merely attached case-marking material.

A postpositions-only slice would risk duplicating the case-marking packet too quickly. A relator-nouns-only slice would hide the main reason the domain matters: relator nouns and separate postpositions meet in the same constructions. The next slice should therefore be combined, but deliberately small and boundary-controlled.
