# Tedim relative-clause packet scope

This dossier organizes the evidence layer for the relative-clause packet, distinguishing true relative-clause diagnostics from nominalization, case-marked nominalization, and ordinary subordination.

## Scope decision

The first grammar-facing print slice should be titled **"`mi`-headed and nominalized relative-like constructions"** (conservative label) or **"Relative and nominalized relative-like clause modifiers"** (less hedged).

The packet remains **narrow**: it does not attempt a full relative-clause system, full nominalization system, full NP structure treatment, full case system, full subordination chapter, or general clause-linkage inventory. Instead, it focuses on the safest candidates for a first grammar-facing slice.

## Evidence organization

### Group 1: Promoted `mi`-headed relative diagnostics

**`a bawl mi` (Exodus 30:38)**

This is the core promoted diagnostic for `mi`-headed relatives:

- Form: `a bawl mi` "person who makes"
- Source: Exodus 30:38 (Bible corpus)
- Tedim text: `paknamtui a bawl mi peuhmah`
- Segmentation: `paknamtui | a | bawl | mi | peuhmah`
- Gloss: `perfume | 3SG.REL | make | person | every`
- Translation: "whoever makes any like it"
- Head noun: `mi` (person)
- Relative clause span: `a bawl` (3SG makes)
- Head role: Patient/Affected (implicit object)
- Subject of relative clause: Implicit agent (agreement prefix `a-`)
- Construction type: Prenominal `a`-VERB + head noun `mi`
- Report evidence: 22 tokens of `a bawl mi` cited in the relative-clause report
- Print status: **PROMOTED** - use as a formal example

**Why promoted:**
- Secure source resolution (Bible corpus, Exodus 30:38)
- Clear prenominal structure
- Transparent head noun (`mi` = person)
- Explicit agent role of the head
- Matches the report's core pattern

**Display:** Formal interlinear example with source reference

### Group 2: Supporting relative-like or headless diagnostics

**`omte` (Genesis 2:1)**

This supporting diagnostic illustrates plural relative-like formation:

- Form: `omte` "those who exist / those that are"
- Source: Genesis 2:1 (Bible corpus)
- Tedim text: `a sunga omte khempeuh`
- Segmentation: `a | sunga | om-te | khempeuh`
- Gloss: `3SG | inside | exist-PL | all`
- Translation: "all that was in it" / "all the existing-ones inside"
- Head noun: None (headless/free relative interpretation)
- Relative clause span: `om` (exists/is)
- Construction type: Headless relative with plural nominalization suffix `-te`
- Report evidence: 73 tokens of `omte` cited in the relative-clause report
- Print status: **SUPPORTING** - include as evidence for plural formation if space permits

**Why supporting:**
- Secure source resolution (Bible corpus, Genesis 2:1)
- Illustrates plural marking in relative-like construction
- Matches the report's plural pattern
- But headless/free relative interpretation is ambiguous with plural nominalization

**Display:** Table row or brief formal example, marked as supporting

**`a om lai` (report-backed, locative relative pattern)**

This corpus-backed pattern illustrates locative relatives:

- Form: `a om lai` "place where (it) is"
- Relative clause span: `a om` (3SG exists/is)
- Head noun: `lai` (place)
- Construction type: Prenominal `a`-VERB + locative head noun
- Report evidence: 133 tokens of `a om lai` cited in the relative-clause report
- Print status: **SUPPORTING if source-resolved, otherwise corpus-backed**

**Why supporting:**
- High frequency in report (133 tokens)
- Matches the report's locative-head pattern
- But no specific Bible verse has yet been identified

**Display:** If a specific source can be resolved, include as a table row or brief example

### Group 3: Boundary material

#### Nominalization boundary

**`omna` (Genesis 4:16)**

This nominalized form belongs with the nominalization section, not the relative-clause core:

- Form: `omna` "place of being / existence"
- Source: Genesis 4:16
- Tedim text: `Topa' omna panin Kain paikhia-in`
- Segmentation: `Topa' | om-na | pan-in | Kain | paikhia-in`
- Gloss: `LORD.POSS | be.exist-NMLZ | ABL-CVB | Kain | paikhia-in`
- Translation: "Cain went out from the presence of the LORD"
- Construction type: Nominalized relative-like form with `-na` nominalizer
- Report evidence: 474 tokens of `omna` cited in the relative-clause report
- Print status: **BOUNDARY ONLY** - keep in nominalization section, do not promote to relative-clause core

**Why boundary:**
- Overlaps with nominalization section (covered under deverbal nominalization with `-na`)
- The nominalization section already treats `-na` as the core nominalizer
- The locative semantics is better understood through nominalization + case interaction
- Already classified as boundary material in the clause-linkage packet

**Cross-reference:** `output/publication_review/grammar_nominalization_print_slice.md` (lines 77-80) explicitly treats nominalized relatives as boundaries

#### Nominalization plus case boundary

**`muhna-ah` (Genesis 6:11)**

This case-marked nominalized form belongs with nominalization + case, not the relative-clause core:

- Form: `muhna-ah` "in (someone's) sight"
- Source: Genesis 6:11
- Tedim text: `Pasian' muhna-ah leitung sia a`
- Segmentation: `Pasian' | muh-na-ah | leitung | sia | a`
- Gloss: `God.GEN | see-NMLZ-LOC | earth | corrupt | DECL`
- Translation: "the earth was corrupt in the sight of God"
- Construction type: Nominalized form with locative case marking `-ah`
- Report evidence: 247 tokens of `muhna` cited in the relative-clause report
- Print status: **BOUNDARY ONLY** - keep with nominalization + case sections, do not promote

**Why boundary:**
- This is explicitly nominalization + case marking, not a relative-clause formation
- The case-marking section may eventually expand to treat such forms
- Already classified as `nominalization_boundary` in the switch-reference packet
- Overlaps with multiple sections rather than being a clean relative-clause diagnostic

**Cross-reference:** `output/publication_review/grammar_nominalization_print_slice.md` (lines 77-80) and potential future case-marking section updates

## Final scope statement

The first grammar-facing print slice keeps:

1. **Promoted**: `a bawl mi` as the core `mi`-headed relative diagnostic
2. **Supporting**: `omte` as plural relative formation evidence; `a om lai` if source-resolved
3. **Boundary**: `omna` and `muhna-ah` (nominalization boundaries); ordinary clause-linkage material (boundary control)
4. **Deferred**: `a nei mi`, `a gen thu`, and other corpus-backed patterns without specific Bible verse resolution

## Example-level verification result

See `output/publication_review/relative_clauses_example_verification.md` for detailed verification of each example. Summary:

- **`a bawl mi` (promoted)**: Verified clean. The prefix `a-` is ordinary 3SG agreement, and `mi` is the head noun. The prenominal structure yields a relative-like reading.
- **`omte` (supporting)**: Verified appropriate. The form is ambiguous between headless/free relative and plural nominalization; supporting status is correct.
- **`omna` (boundary)**: Verified as nominalization boundary material. The `-na` nominalizer creates a place/presence frame, not a relative clause.
- **`muhna-ah` (boundary)**: Verified as case-marked nominalization boundary material. The form combines nominalization (`-na`) with locative case (`-ah`).
- **Clause-linkage boundaries**: Verified to remain outside the relative-clause core.
