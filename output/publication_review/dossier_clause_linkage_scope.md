# Candidate groups

## Subordination

`Ciangin` is the cleanest current subordination anchor. It is the safest future print-facing row because the reports and literature review converge on it as a major temporal subordinator.

`Dingin` is also important and should stay explicit in the packet. The reports support it as clause-bound purposive or irrealis material, but its status still overlaps with TAM enough that it should remain candidate-controlled rather than leading the first slice by itself.

Broader report inventory such as `hangin` and `bangin` remains useful discovery material, but it is not as clean for the first packet as `ciangin`.

## Switch reference

The switch-reference evidence is real, but it is less print-ready than the temporal subordination evidence.

The current packet keeps two rows explicit:

1. `VERB-in`, using `ngenin` as the anchor example for same-subject chaining;
2. `ahih ciangin` as the clearest different-subject construction now visible in the reports.

Both are worth keeping in the candidate layer, but both remain more theory-heavy than `ciangin`. The reports and literature review still leave open whether some of this material is best treated as converbial chaining, switch reference, or broader subordination.

## Relative clauses

Relative-clause evidence exists, but it is the most boundary-heavy part of the packet.

`A bawl mi` is the clearest current prenominal relative-clause row, and `omna` is the clearest nominalized-relative row. Both are good enough to keep in the candidate layer, but neither is as safe as `ciangin` for a first print-facing slice.

`Muhna-ah` remains boundary-only. It shows that clause linkage also interacts with nominalization and case routing, but it belongs to a later more explicit nominalization-plus-case treatment.

## Cross-packet boundary rows

The packet also needs a few explicit cross-packet boundaries:

- `dingin` as the main TAM/subordination overlap row;
- `leh` as the main sentence-final-particle or subordinator boundary row;
- `a bawl mi` as the clearest prefix/agreement or relativizer-like boundary row.

Those rows matter because clause linkage is not isolated from the rest of the grammar architecture. The packet therefore has to identify the boundaries now instead of pretending they do not exist.

# Existing packet boundaries

Clause linkage has to stay narrow against several completed packets.

- `output/publication_review/review_notes_sentence_final_particles.md` is boundary control against treating ambiguous clause-final material as already solved sentence-particle territory.
- `output/publication_review/review_notes_tam.md` is boundary control for `dingin` and other clause-bound irrealis or purposive material that cannot simply be absorbed into TAM.
- `output/publication_review/review_notes_vp_structure_stacking.md` is boundary control against turning converbial or chaining evidence into a new VP-stacking claim.
- `output/publication_review/review_notes_prefix_agreement.md` is boundary control for `a-` or relativizer-like material that overlaps with prefix/agreement analysis.
- `output/publication_review/review_notes_pronouns.md` is boundary control against reopening independent-pronoun or clusivity work while discussing switch-reference or relativizer-like forms.

These boundaries explain why the packet is still only at the candidate/scoping stage.

# Deferred material

Several important rows should remain deferred for now.

- `hangin` and `bangin` remain broad report inventory rather than first print anchors.
- `dingin` remains accepted only with caveat because purposive or irrealis material still overlaps with TAM.
- `VERB-in` remains accepted only with caveat because the literature review still leaves open how sharply to separate converb, subordinator, and switch-reference analyses.
- `leh` remains deferred because conditional, coordinator, and inconclusive sentence-particle analyses still overlap.
- `muhna-ah` remains deferred because nominalization-plus-case routing is not yet stable enough for the first slice.
- relative-clause counts involving `a-` remain theoretically unsettled enough that the packet should not yet generalize from them into a full relativizer chapter.

The packet should also explicitly avoid analyzer-noisy or report-only rows that do not improve the first print-facing claim.
